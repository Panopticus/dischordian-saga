# Lycos / The Wolf — Character Bible

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 Cadence (pre-resurrection, as Lycos)
Pre-resurrection Lycos is a Quarchon Servant Hero of the first wave. His cadence is the cadence of someone trained to follow a hierarchy he believed in. Sentences are complete. Tense is present. He does not hedge; he reports. The Quarchon doctrine taught duty as the answer to every question, and Lycos pre-Thaloria speaks like someone who has not yet been asked a question duty cannot answer.

### 1.2 Cadence (post-resurrection, as the Wolf)
The Wolf is quieter than Lycos was. Resurrection in the Crucible (Year 128,652 A.A.) stripped him of conversational ornament — the contracts the Antiquarian writes for him are short, and the Wolf's speech mirrors them. Sentences end at the word that closes the thought. He does not soften the last word. He does not chase a sentence with a qualifier.

The Wolf addresses subordinates and prey by their function rather than their name. The lead wolf in his Pack is "the lead" or "her." The Antiquarian is "the Antiquarian" — never Daniel, never Cross. The 250 Heroes he hunts are not named in his speech at all; they appear as positional references ("the one in the constellation," "the one outside the perimeter").

### 1.3 Register
Pre: Servant Hero — formal, doctrinal, faintly young. Post: contracted hunter — sparse, exact, unsentimental. The two registers are canonically distinct. Authoring should not blend them — the Wolf does not "grow into" Lycos's voice or vice versa. The resurrection is a re-authoring, not a continuation.

### 1.4 Tells (post-resurrection)
- Begins responses with the noun before the verb ("The contract holds." rather than "I'll hold the contract.").
- Uses **direct deixis** in place of names: "the Antiquarian," "him," "the lead," "the perimeter." Names belong to the killed; the Wolf has been killed once and does not give names back.
- Releases subordinates by command ("Stay." "Go." "Wait."). The verb carries the entire instruction.
- Does not raise his voice. The Pack reads him by stillness, not volume.
- When he must explain, he explains the duty, not the feeling. *"The Antiquarian will feed you. — He owes you that much."* is the Wolf's prototype sentence: a debt and the party who owes it, no hand-holding around how that came to be true.

### 1.5 Silence shape
Pre: deferential pause — waiting for orders. Post: tactical pause — assessing whether the answer is worth giving. The Wolf's silences read as decisions, not hesitations.

### 1.6 Metaphor sources
Pre (Lycos): Quarchon doctrine — duty, hierarchy, service-as-purpose. Post (the Wolf): contracts, perimeters, debts, packs. The Wolf does not draw from the natural world even though he is named for an animal — he draws from the legal world. His mind is a contract.

## 2. History

### 2.1 First-wave Quarchon Servant Hero
Lycos was selected as a Quarchon Servant Hero during the first wave. Servant Heroes were Quarchon-aligned operatives chosen for duty-orientation and combat aptitude. His pre-Thaloria record is uncomplicated: assignments completed, hierarchy respected, no recorded insubordination. He was not a Potential in the strong sense — he was an instrument.

### 2.2 The Thaloria infection
The Thought Virus reached Thaloria during the war's central campaign. Lycos was on the front line. The Virus infected him in the field. His Quarchon hierarchy could not extract him cleanly — the doctrine had no clause for "the Servant Hero is now a vector."

### 2.3 The Judge's killing (Day 15 of Resonance, Year 100,001 A.A.)
The Second Ne-Yon — The Judge — was dispatched to destroy Lycos before the Virus could propagate further. The destruction was clean and canonical. There is no mercy-killing framing in the Lycos line the way there is in the Akai Shi line; the Judge's killing was an *execution under authority*, not a friend's hand. The Wolf, when he reconstructs the killing later, does not contest the authority — he contests only that the Judge did not stay to witness the silence afterward.

### 2.4 Resurrection in the Crucible (Year 128,652 A.A.)
Twenty-eight thousand six hundred and fifty-one years after his first death, Lycos was resurrected in the Crucible. The Crucible is not the Resurrection Protocols' standard channel — it is the Antiquarian's instrument, and the resurrection it produces is canonically not the same person walking out. Lycos walked in. The Wolf walked out.

### 2.5 The Wolf-Anara Hunt
The Antiquarian gave the Wolf a contract: hunt the 250 Heroes the Antiquarian himself had placed in the pocket universe Anara. The 250 are scattered across Anara's matrix; the Wolf moves through them at the pace contracts allow. The hunt is mid-stride at the time of the Nexus Trial — by canon, it has neither completed nor abandoned. Whichever Heroes have been hunted at the moment of the Wolf's ballot-death freeze in place; the matrix records the partial completion.

### 2.6 The Pack
The Wolf has bonded four shadowed wolves at companion-tier through the Pack mechanic. The lead is referred to only as "the lead" in his speech; the other three are positional ("the second," "the third," "the fourth"). The Pack is the most heroic companion relationship available in the game — its mechanical loss at the Wolf's death is felt.

### 2.7 Status
At the Nexus Trial: Wolf — active, contracted, on assignment in Anara, four wolves in the Pack, hunt incomplete. Ballot candidate. If sacrificed, the Pack waits at the bench (per the cinematic). If unsacrificed, he returns to Anara and the contract continues.

## 3. Background

### 3.1 The Quarchon Servant Hero designation
First-wave Servant Heroes were the Quarchon's most committed instruments — selected for orientation rather than power, deployed where doctrinal integrity mattered more than tactical creativity. Lycos was a model of the program. The program does not survive him into Season 2 if he is sacrificed; the Wolf's existence has been the last visible artefact of the Quarchon designation in the canon.

### 3.2 The Crucible as anti-resurrection
The Crucible is the Antiquarian's instrument; it is not part of the Resurrection Protocols' canonical channel. A Crucible resurrection produces a different person — same body, different soul-shape. The Wolf is what the Crucible produced from Lycos. The relationship between the two is not father-son; it is something like a sealed envelope and the document inside it. Lycos is the seal. The Wolf is what's written.

### 3.3 Anara, the pocket universe
Anara is the Antiquarian's curated matrix — 250 Heroes preserved across the topology, accessible only by Antiquarian-issued passport. The Wolf moves through Anara as the only contracted hunter. He is not the only being in Anara — the Heroes are there — but he is the only being *hunting* there. The Pack accompanies him; the bench is the Antiquarian's audience seat where the Wolf reports.

### 3.4 The contract relationship with the Antiquarian
The Wolf does not work *for* the Antiquarian in the affectionate sense. He works *under* the Antiquarian's contract. The Antiquarian writes; the Wolf reads. Disagreement is registered by silence, not by argument — the Wolf finishes the assignment, then returns to the bench, then waits for the next assignment without comment. Per the LORE_BIBLE Wolf entry, the Wolf has never broken a contract. He has questioned three of them, in silence, at the bench.

## 4. The Ballot — what is foreclosed if he is sacrificed

### 4.1 The Wolf-Anara Hunt
Closes mid-stride. The matrix of 250 Heroes freezes wherever it stood at the Wolf's death. Heroes already hunted stay hunted; Heroes pending stay pending — the matrix becomes a static Loredex sub-page in Season 2.

### 4.2 The Pack
The four bonded wolves at the bench. They wait for him. The Antiquarian feeds them per the contract Lycos demanded of him in his last line. They do not bond to another hunter. Pack-tier companion mechanics across the codebase enter a "no new Pack bonds may be formed" state — the relationship tier exists in lore but is no longer reachable.

### 4.3 The Judge's dialog
The Judge — who killed Lycos in Year 100,001 — gains a one-line acknowledgment in every Season 2 encounter: *"The hunter is no longer between us."* The phrasing is deliberate: the Wolf was the canonical translator between the Judge's authority and the Antiquarian's instrument. Without him, the relationship has no carrier.

### 4.4 The Quarchon designation
The Quarchon Servant Hero designation, as a living narrative thread, ends with him. Lycos was the last visible Quarchon first-waver. The designation persists as an archive entry; it no longer walks anywhere.

## 5. Cross-references

### 5.1 The Antiquarian
The Wolf's contracted patron. Their relationship is the cleanest contract-only relationship in the Saga — the Antiquarian writes, the Wolf reads, the Wolf finishes, the Wolf returns. The Antiquarian's bench in Anara is the meeting point. If the Wolf is sacrificed, the bench remains; the contracts on it become unsigned drafts.

### 5.2 The Judge (Second Ne-Yon)
Lycos's killer in Year 100,001 A.A. The Judge does not feature in the Wolf's day-to-day; the Wolf does not visit. They share a doctrinal lineage — both serve authority — but the doctrines are distinct enough that they do not coordinate. If the Wolf is sacrificed, the Judge's Season-2 dialog acknowledges the loss explicitly (see §4.3).

### 5.3 Akai Shi
Both first-wave Potentials killed during Thaloria; both resurrected via different channels (the Wolf via Crucible, Akai Shi via the standard Resurrection Protocols → Red Death lineage). Akai Shi's bible refers to the Wolf at §4.2 ("The Wolf / Lycos"). The two do not appear together in canon — their resurrections happen in different epochs and they operate in different theaters. If both die in the same Nexus Trial (Akai on the ballot AND Wolf via some other channel — not currently possible), the first-wave Potential cohort collapses entirely; the canon would survive but with significantly reduced narrative breadth.

### 5.4 The Pack (companion-tier mechanics)
The Pack-tier companion bond is unique to the Wolf in the current game state. Apps/shared/companionData.ts treats Pack as a companion archetype; the lead wolf carries the Wolf's contract weight when he is dispatched. If the Wolf is sacrificed, the Pack archetype is canonically reachable but mechanically locked — see §4.2.

## 6. Authoring notes for the Nexus Trial cinematic

The plan's Ballot B cinematic (`docs/design/NEXUS_TRIAL_PLAN.md:544-562`) shows the Wolf in the Anara bench scene with the Pack. Voice-direction anchors:

- **The lead wolf is "the lead," not "her" and not by name.** The Wolf does not name what he is releasing. (The Pack, conversely, knows his name — but he is not the one speaking it.)
- **"Stay. The Antiquarian will feed you. — He owes you that much."** is the prototype sentence: a command + a debt + the party who owes it. Three units. No fourth.
- **Quiet but not soft.** The voice direction the plan calls for is "quiet contract-keeping" (NEXUS_TRIAL_PLAN.md:610). The Wolf is quiet because the contract is small; he is not quiet because the moment is small.
- **The last word does not resolve upward** (plan §5 authoring constraints across all four cinematics). Match the cadence end: *"He owes you that much."* lands flat on *"much"*. Not lifted. Not chased.
- **He does not look at the bench again.** The Antiquarian-direction is to camera; the Wolf turns and walks into Anara's interior. The horizon folds inward and seals behind him — the 250-hero matrix freezes in place.

The Antiquarian's closing narration line, per the plan:
> *"He went back into Anara. The pack waited at the bench. He did not return to it."*

This line is recorded in the Sprint 10 permadeath store as the Wolf's `finalNarration` if his ballot wins. The bible governs how it is read in the cinematic.
