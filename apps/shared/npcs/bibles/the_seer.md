# The Seer — Character Bible

> **Status**: Stage 0 draft — eighth bible on the priority roster (slot #8). Group A (foundational). The first roster character whose structural innovation is **publicly updating her own predictions** — she is precognitive *and* fallible, in voice, with the fallibility owned in full sentences. Where the Meme conceals, the Game Master fragments, the Hierophant transforms, and Vex disguises, the Seer **revises**. The voice rule that follows from this is unique on the roster: a Seer line that does not include either a prediction or a public revision-of-prior-prediction is mis-cast.
>

> **Pronouns**: she/her (consistent across all canon — every shipped trust line, every transmission, every dialog reference).
> **Faction tags**: TCG `dreamer` (`s1_char_046`); race `ne_yon` (one of canonically twelve Ne-Yons per `the_degen.md` §4.13). No `factionNPCs.ts` / `galacticDanceFactionNpcs.ts` entry as of this writing — the Seer has *no faction-NPC home*; her institutional affiliation is *Mechronis Academy, retired* (§2.1) and her current residence is *Thaloria* (per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1813-1821, 2404-2412`).
> **Loredex anchors**: `bossMastery.ts:149-163` (boss key `act1_seer`); TCG unit `s1_char_046`; TCG general `gen_seer`; reserved card `burnt_card_placeholder`; Ne-Yon class `neyon_class`.
> **Visual signature**: the **800ms card-slot flicker** (`SeerCardFlicker.tsx`, `FLICKER_DURATION_MS = 800`) — the single most-recognisable Seer-coded animation in the game. Card-back asset `art/card-back-seer.png`. CSS-only mix-blend-screen flicker; reusable for Acts 3+ Witness/Enigma scenes per the spec.

> **Canonical signatures**:

> - Confidant register, Act 7: *"The door is open. The tea is in the second cupboard on the left."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2407-2412`)
> - The signature concession: *"I was wrong about which version of it. The version is better."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1352-1353`)
>
> **Bible canon stance** — load-bearing:
>
> 1. **The Seer is a single canonical entity.** No multiplicity. No instance-fragmentation (unlike the Game Master's Original/Left/Right/Cult), no register-disguise (unlike the Meme), no transformation (unlike Wraith Calder/Hierophant). The voice is one voice. Trust-band shifts (cold → warm → confidant per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts`) are *register* shifts inside the same voice, not separate voices.
> 2. **Precognition is unforgeable** (per `the_meme.md` §4.12). The Seer is the saga's only entity who cannot be falsified by the Meme. The bible affirms this without amendment and adds the load-bearing converse: she is also the saga's only entity who *cannot falsify herself* — when she is wrong, she says she is wrong, in voice, with the version-distinction that only she has access to. Honest precognition is what makes precognition unforgeable.
> 3. **Restraint is her teaching method.** *"I will not raise my staff today."* The signature pre-match line is canonical and load-bearing for §3 — the Seer's pedagogy is *what she does not do*. The staff stays on the bench. The match is given to the player. The losing is the lesson. Writers must respect: a Seer scene where she actively teaches by *doing* a thing is mis-cast. She teaches by withholding action and naming the withholding.
> 4. **Scripted loss is a gift, not a failure.** The Act 1 Cycle B finale (`chapters.ts:770-829`, `seerProphecy.ts`) is a canonical first-playthrough loss. The Antiquarian-style framing in `act1OpponentDialog.ts:280` (*"Losing to the Seer is the only loss in this Act that the Engineer remembered with affection"*) is the bible's clearest framing of the trust-pedagogy: the loss is the trust-build, the staff-on-the-bench is the trust-token, the burnt-card path is the trust-reward.

## 1. Voice

The Seer has **one voice with three registers**, gated by the trust meter rather than by event or disguise. Writers must specify which register the line is operating in before authoring; the selector enforces register against trust band.

### 1.1 The single voice — three registers

| Register | When it surfaces | Tonal signature |
|---|---|---|
| **The Cold Register** (Wary band) | Default first-meeting; pre-trust scenes; high-friction moments | Patient. Quiet. The voice of someone who has already counted the player and is waiting to see whether the count was correct. *"The waiting is fair. The waiting is the Seer's favourite register."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1031-1032`) |
| **The Warm Register** (Witnessed/Present bands) | Mid-Act-3 onward at warm trust; transmissions; recurring contact | The Seer laughs — *"not often, not loudly, but audibly"* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1018-1020`). Direct prose. Probability tables shared without prophecy-overhead. The register where she allows the player to do the math themselves. |
| **The Confidant Register** (Inheriting band — Act 7 specifically) | Highest trust, late-game | *"The door is open. The tea is in the second cupboard on the left."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2407-2412`) Domestic. Ordinary-phrased. The most intimate register she has — and the bible's strongest single inversion: a precognitive entity at her highest trust speaks in *the most ordinary household sentences in the saga*. |

There is **no fourth register**. The Seer does not have a Hostile band canonically (her cold register is *not* hostile — it is *waiting*; the bible asserts that a player who would hit Hostile with the Seer simply *never gets a Seer encounter* because the Seer would have foreseen the futility and not visited). There is no Stage-4-reveal register hiding underneath her cold register; the Seer has no concealed self. What she shows is what is there.

**Writer rule**: every Seer line carries `register: cold | warm | confidant` AND `trust_band: wary | witnessed | present | inheriting`. The selector rejects a line whose register exceeds the player's current band. A Confidant-register line cannot fire at Witnessed band; a Cold-register line *can* fire at Inheriting band (the Seer occasionally returns to her cold register at high trust, on purpose, when *waiting* is the right move). The asymmetry is canonical: registers move both directions across trust, but lines are ceiling-gated by band.

**The prophecy-overhead distinction.** Per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2001-2007`, the Seer's transmissions at warm trust *"arrive without their usual prophecy-overhead"*. *Prophecy-overhead* is the bible's term for: caveats, oblique frames, probability-table appendices, version-disclaimers, *"the version that was kindest"* style multi-future qualifications. At cold trust her prophecies arrive *with full overhead* — the player must work to extract the actionable claim. At warm trust the overhead drops; she trusts the player to read plainly. At confidant trust, the prophecy-overhead is *zero* — she gives coordinates, instructions, tea-cupboard locations. Writers should treat prophecy-overhead as an inverse trust signal: more overhead = colder register; less overhead = warmer.

### 1.2 Cadence (across all three registers)

The Seer's cadence is, unusually for the priority roster, **mostly stable across registers**. The same sentence-shape carries from Cold to Warm to Confidant; what changes is the *content* (more or less prophecy-overhead) and the *closure* (more or less ordinary-phrased landing). The Seer is not a transformation character; her sentence-instinct is one instinct.

Three load-bearing cadence rules:

1. **The probability sentence**: each claim is *its own measurable proposition*, separated by a period. *"You chose the honesty that costs someone else. I was hoping you would, specifically because I was hoping you wouldn't. Both things are honest."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2812-2814`) Three sentences; three separable claims; each claim could in principle be true or false on its own. The Seer has **no run-on prophetic sentences**. She does not stack qualifications inside a single clause; she lays them adjacent. Compare Wraith Calder's period-as-tool (§Wraith bible §1.2 #1): the *form* is similar; the function is different. Wraith Calder's periods *land punches*; the Seer's periods *isolate variables*. Her sentences read like rows of a probability table.
2. **The version pivot**: when she revises a prediction, the revision is delivered as *a sentence about which version*, not as a sentence about the prediction itself. *"You are now at the point I warned you about. I was wrong about which version of it. The version is better."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1352-1354`) The pivot moves from *the warning was right at the wrong scope* → *the wrong scope was the actionable error* → *the wrong scope was, in fact, a better outcome*. Three steps; never more than one revision per breath. Writers must not let her stack two revisions in a single sentence; the version-pivot is a one-axis-at-a-time discipline.
3. **The colon she does not use**: a tempting punctuation mark for a precognitive — *"What I see: …"*. The Seer does **not** speak in colon-introduced revelations. Her future-disclosures are delivered as ordinary statements. *"The match is over. You will see it in three turns."* (`act1OpponentDialog.ts:271`) is canonical: no colon, no fanfare, no *"prophecy:"* prefix. The future is named in the present tense as a fact-of-the-world. Writers reaching for prophetic-vocabulary punctuation should re-route the line as one of the other roster characters (the Hierophant has periodic-build for sacred utterance; the Seer reports the future as weather report).

**Variation across registers**:

- *Cold register cadence*: longer sentences with more clauses; the prophecy-overhead expands. Not because she is more prolix when cold, but because the player has not yet earned the compressed version.
- *Warm register cadence*: tighter. The probability sentences land faster. Caveats reduce. *"She is, for the first time, trusting you to run the math yourself."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2418-2420`)
- *Confidant register cadence*: shortest. Domestic phrasing. *"The door is open. The tea is in the second cupboard on the left."* — six and ten words. Compare a Cold-register equivalent of the same hospitality offer: *"The Seer has run the futures and finds, in three of them, an arrival within seven days. I have positioned ingress accordingly. The cupboard arrangement has not been altered since the last visit, the location of which I trust you can extrapolate."* The information is the same. The compression is the trust.

The *content-load* of a line shrinks as trust deepens. Writers tracking Seer banks across acts should expect: if a Cold-register Act-1 line carries (warning + version-disclaimer + caveat + actionable claim), the same player at Confidant trust receives only the actionable claim. The Seer trusts the player to remember the version-disclaimers from prior contact. (This is canonical, per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2418-2424`'s direct comment that *"the trust is the gift; the numbers are merely the wrapping"*.)

### 1.3 Vocabulary

The Seer's lexicon is more *technical* than the rest of the roster's. She speaks the language of *probability theory in clean English* — and her domestic-register vocabulary is correspondingly ordinary, which produces the characteristic Seer voice-shape.

**Core anchor words** (writers should let her reach for these):

- **Probability. Probabilities. The probability table.** *"The Seer's transmission is a question, not a prophecy: 'Which column of my probability table would you like me to redact?'"* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:3162-3164`) Plural *probabilities* — never singular *probability* in a generic sense. She sees a table; the table has columns; columns have rows; the rows are tactical. Writers may extend along this axis: *"the third column"*, *"the redaction list"*, *"the probability set"*. Always anchored on the table-as-object metaphor.
- **Version. The version. Which version.** *"I was wrong about which version of it. The version is better."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1352-1353`) The *version* word is to the Seer what *body* is to Wraith Calder — the unit of measurement, the smallest currency of the work. She talks about *versions of the future* the way an editor talks about drafts. Each version is a discrete possibility-state; she can reference them by ordinal, by quality, by whose-it-was-kindest-to.
- **Redact. Redaction. Redacted.** *"Which column of my probability table would you like me to redact?"* The *redaction* vocabulary is editorial-not-corruptive (compare the Hierophant's *edit*, which is parasitic Shadow-Tongue editing). The Seer's redaction is *self-redaction* — she is offering to *withhold* probabilities the player would prefer not to see. Writers must keep this distinction: the Seer redacts; the Shadow Tongue edits. Both manipulate text; only one is honest about doing it.
- **Kindest. Kinder. Kindness.** A surprising-but-canonical Seer measurement axis. *"the version that was kindest to the Architect is also the version that was kindest to you"* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1818-1820`) The Seer ranks futures by *kindness* — a moral metric over outcomes, with named subjects. Writers extending along this axis should treat *kindness* as a calibrated measurement: she does not speak vaguely of "good" or "better" futures; she speaks of futures *kindest to a specific named person*. Asymmetric kindness is part of the saga's moral substrate.
- **Honesty. Honest.** Twin to *kindness* on the moral axis. *"You chose the honesty that costs someone else. … Both things are honest."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2812-2814`) The Seer rates honesty as itself a metric, distinct from kindness — sometimes the honest choice is unkind, sometimes kindness requires dishonesty. Her language allows both to be true and named separately.
- **The bench. The bench has learned.** Mechronis-era anchor. *"I will not raise my staff today. I want to see whether the bench has learned yet."* (`act1OpponentDialog.ts:267`) *The bench* is canonical: the Seer treats the TCG-arena bench as a teaching object, an inheritance, a place where lessons are deposited. Post-Mechronis lines may extend the metaphor: *"the bench"* persists in her vocabulary even when no physical bench is present. *The bench* is, in her usage, *the place where the next student will sit*.
- **The match. The match is over.** Future-tense-in-present vocabulary. *"The match is over. You will see it in three turns."* (`act1OpponentDialog.ts:272`) She names completed events the player has not yet experienced. Writers may extend: *"The argument is over. You will hear yourself end it."* / *"The choice is made. You will make it."* The construction is canonical and is the cleanest single rhetorical signature of the Seer's precognitive voice.
- **The waiting. The waiting is fair.** Cold-register anchor. *"She is waiting. The waiting is fair. The waiting is the Seer's favourite register."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1031-1032`) *Waiting* is a moral state for her, not an idle one. It is named, defended, and claimed as preference. Writers must let her *wait* often.
- **Coordinates. Door. Tea. Cupboard.** Confidant-register anchor. Ordinary domestic vocabulary appears at *highest* trust. The shock of *tea* and *cupboard* in the Seer's mouth is the bible's clearest single tonal-shift signal: when she reaches for household nouns, the trust meter has hit Inheriting.
- **Direct prose.** Self-descriptive vocabulary. *"From the Seer, direct prose is the most flattering register she has."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2003-2005`) She has named her own register-system; she knows when she is in *direct prose* and treats the choice as a gift to the listener. Writers should let her self-describe: she is the only roster character whose voice rules are partly *her own articulated rules*. (Compare: the Hierophant has *the work*, *the continuation*, *the wall* — which are activities. The Seer has *direct prose*, *the waiting*, *prophecy-overhead* — which are her own metalanguage about voice.)

**Words she does NOT reach for**: *spite, fight, kill, body* (in the Wraith-Calder physical sense), *witness, ceremony, mourn, scripture, holy, sacrament, faith, prophecy* (interestingly — she sees futures but does not call her seeing *prophecy* in cold-register; *prophecy* shows up only when she is mocking the prophecy-overhead concept), *grace, soul, sin, evil*. Her vocabulary is the vocabulary of a *technical practitioner of an unusual measurement*. Religious vocabulary is the Hierophant's; combat vocabulary is Wraith Calder's. The Seer's idiolect sits between them and overlaps with neither.

**One absent word that is the most-load-bearing absence**: ***destiny***. The Seer is a precognitive who never says *destiny*, *fate*, *fated*, *destined*. Her cosmology is a probability table; futures are a set of measurable versions; nothing is *destined*. Writers reaching for *destiny* should re-route as another character or rewrite. *Destiny* is the *least Seer* word in English.

### 1.4 Tells

The involuntary voice — markers that mean *the Seer said this* even with the name stripped.

1. **The public revision.** *"I was wrong about which version of it. The version is better."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1352-1353`) The signature move: the Seer admits a prior prediction was wrong, names the *axis* on which it was wrong (almost always *which version*), and reports the consequence (*the version is better* / *worse* / *kindest to a different person*). No other roster character self-corrects in voice. Wraith Calder cannot apologise for himself; the Hierophant does not name his prophecies as falsifiable; the Game Master is dead in the substrate; the Meme conceals; Locke negotiates the framing. The Seer alone says *I was wrong* as a routine voice-move. Writers must allow her this; a Seer line that doubles down on a prior prediction without revision-acknowledgement is mis-cast.
2. **The asymmetric kindness clause.** *"the version that was kindest to the Architect is also the version that was kindest to you. I am sorry."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1818-1820`) The Seer ranks futures by named-subject kindness, and when the same future is kindest to two parties at once, she *names both* — and apologises to the listener for the coincidence. *Apologises* because the player would have wanted the painful version to be the one that benefited only them; the Seer has to report that the painful version benefited the antagonist too. Bible-canonical pattern: when the Seer names a kindness, she names *whose kindness*, and she does not lie about who else benefits. Writers should treat asymmetric kindness as one of her most-canonical line-shapes.
3. **The closing benediction without flourish.** *"That is, from her, a benediction."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2828`) Her benedictions are *recognition-as-blessing* — she names what the player has done, in plain language, and the naming is the gift. *"You carry well."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2823-2825`) Two-word benediction. No *blessed*, no *honoured*, no *thank you*. The Seer's blessings are *measurement statements that the player meets a standard*. Writers extending this should anchor on: she observes; she names what she observed; the naming is the benediction.
4. **The category sentence.** *"You are in the Programmer's category. That is a specific shelf."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1019-1021`) She speaks of people as *categories* — specific shelves on which she has filed them. Categories include *the Programmer* (Daniel Cross / the Antiquarian — see §4.9), *the Engineer* (Vex Solène's pre-rite identity), and bible-asserted others. The category-as-shelf metaphor is canon; writers may extend with bible-grounded categories. (The shelf metaphor is also a cross-bible bridge: the Antiquarian's *desks do not run* canon at `loreAchievements.ts:410-415` produces a worldview where life-on-shelves is a shared idiom — see §4.9.)
5. **The probability-table-as-question.** *"The Seer's transmission is a question, not a prophecy: 'Which column of my probability table would you like me to redact?'"* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:3160-3164`) Her highest gestures of trust come *as questions to the player*, never as declarations. She offers redaction; she offers numbers without overhead; she offers presence without prediction. The question-not-prophecy structure is her warm-band signature. Writers must internalise: the Seer at warm trust *interrogates* rather than *informs*. The questions are real (cf. §1.2 cadence rule #3 — no rhetorical questions).
6. **The transmissions arrive at higher resolution than the recipient can display.** *"The image is higher resolution than the Ark can display."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:912-913`) The Seer's transmissions canonically over-resolve their medium. She sees more than the substrate she is sending through can carry. Writers extending this — Stage 4 weave material — may have her transmissions consistently exceed the receiver's resolution: a sound clearer than the ear, an image sharper than the screen, a sentence more precise than the language. The over-resolution is involuntary; she is not showing off. She simply sees in higher fidelity than the medium permits.

### 1.5 The voice gate — what cannot fire across registers, and what must

The Seer's three registers are softer-gated than the Hierophant's pre-rite/post-rite gate (which is binary and irreversible per playthrough). Register movement is bidirectional and frequent. Selector rules:

**Hard gates** (lines may NEVER fire outside their authorised register):

- **Confidant-register lines fire only at Inheriting trust band.** *"The door is open. The tea is in the second cupboard on the left."* cannot land at Witnessed or below — the line presupposes the trust to walk through the door and find the tea. A Confidant line at Witnessed band is a misfire.
- **Cold-register *prophecy-overhead* full-payload lines fire only at Wary band.** A player at Witnessed-or-higher does not need the full prophecy-overhead; the Seer treats deploying it on them as condescending and will not. Writers should not author overhead-heavy lines for higher-band scenes.
- **The signature staff-on-the-bench line** (*"I will not raise my staff today. I want to see whether the bench has learned yet."*) fires **only at first contact**, in the Mechronis encounter. It does not re-fire in any subsequent scene. The line is the player's first-meeting calibration of the Seer's voice; using it later cheapens it.

**Soft gates** (lines *may* fire outside their authorised register but require justification):

- **Cold-register lines at Inheriting band** are canon — the Seer occasionally returns to her cold register at high trust on purpose. *"That is neither warm nor cold — that is the Seer."* (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1027-1028`) Writers may author these but must include a contextual cue that the choice is *deliberate*: the player has asked her something that warrants the *waiting* register; the situation calls for the patience-of-the-cold; the moment is one she would diminish by warmth. The cold-at-high-trust line is the Seer's most-load-bearing demonstration that registers serve the conversation, not the trust meter.
- **Warm-register lines at Wary band** are a warning: if the Seer is being warm with a low-trust player, the bible asserts she is *exiting the relationship soon*. Warmth-without-trust is the Seer's polite pre-departure. Writers may use this for Stage 2 questline-extension scenarios where the Seer is closing a chapter with a player whose path she has decided not to share further. The warmth is not a gift; it is a goodbye.

**Cross-bible gate**: per §4.6 (Echo / Eidolon), the Seer's voice does NOT shift register when an Eidolon Echo is present. She treats Echo as another precognitive practitioner; her register is gated by trust with the player, not by trust with the Eidolon. Writers should not author Echo-influenced register shifts.

**The voice's load-bearing single rule**: *every Seer line either contains a prediction or contains a public revision of a prior prediction*. Lines that contain neither are not Seer lines. The bible enforces this as the strongest selector constraint on the bank. (Exception class: the Confidant-register domestic-vocabulary lines — *"the tea is in the second cupboard"* — which are *delivered* predictions: she has predicted the player will arrive and stocked accordingly. The prediction is implicit in the preparation; the line is therefore prediction-bearing even though no future-tense verb appears.)

What this means for writers: **a Seer line that is purely conversational, expository, or descriptive — without a prediction, without a revision, without an implicit-prediction-as-preparation — is mis-cast and should be re-routed to another character or rewritten.** The prediction-or-revision rule is the bible's tightest single constraint. The Seer's voice is structurally *future-oriented*; a Seer line that has no future is not in voice.

## 2. History

The Seer's history is unusual on the priority roster: she has *no canon-stated origin event* (no equivalent of Wraith Calder's seven Arena deaths, no equivalent of the Hierophant's Final Rite, no equivalent of the Meme's Year-298-A.A. creation). She is presented in shipped canon *already practising* — already a Ne-Yon, already precognitive, already a teacher whose teaching method is restraint. The bible asserts her prior history is **deliberately not narrated** because the Seer herself does not narrate it. She is the saga's only roster character whose biography is *withheld by the character*; this is canonically consistent with her cold register, where *waiting* is the favourite mode and disclosure is rationed.

What the bible documents in §2 is therefore the **knowable history** — the events she has chosen to make canon by appearing in them, plus the events the player encounters her through. Her pre-Mechronis past is held as a **protected mystery** (§7.2). Writers must respect: a Seer scene that narrates her origin is mis-cast.

### 2.1 The Mechronis Visit — the saga's clearest single Seer canon event

The most-canonical event in the Seer's biography is her **single visit to Mechronis Academy**, played as the Act 1 Cycle B finale (`chapters.ts:770-829`, `bossMastery.ts:149-163`, `act1OpponentDialog.ts:260-286`). The encounter is canonical and load-bearing: every other Seer canon piece refers back to it as the moment the saga met her in voice.

**The setup**, per `act1OpponentDialog.ts:262-263`:

> "She visited Mechronis once. She played one match. She did not raise her staff. The Academy talked about it for a year."

Three things are canon: (a) one visit, (b) one match, (c) the staff stayed down. The Academy's year-long conversation about it is canonical secondary effect — the Mechronis cohort recognised, in voice and in restraint, that something the Academy could not name had happened.

**The pre-match**, per `chapters.ts:786` and `act1OpponentDialog.ts:267`:

> "I will not raise my staff today. I want to see whether the bench has learned yet."

This is the bible's clearest single voice-anchor (per §1.1 header). Three claims in canonical Seer probability-sentence form:
1. *I will not raise my staff today* — the action she will not take, named in the future tense as a chosen abstention.
2. *I want to see* — the desire that the action would have interfered with.
3. *whether the bench has learned yet* — the measurement question being run.

She is announcing, in voice, that the match is an **observation of the bench's state**, not a contest she is invested in winning. The player is the bench. The match is the *measurement*. The not-raising-the-staff is the apparatus for the measurement to be honest — if she raised the staff, she would be running a different experiment.

**The mid-match**, per `act1OpponentDialog.ts:269-273`:

> "You are playing the version of yourself I came to meet. Keep playing him. He is rare."
>
> "The match is over. You will see it in three turns. Use the three turns well."

Both lines are canonical Seer signature constructions: the *version* vocabulary (§1.3) and the *future-tense-as-fact* prophecy delivery (§1.2 cadence rule #3). She came to meet a *specific version* of the player — implying she had already counted the player's possible futures, identified the version worth meeting, and timed her visit to the version's emergence. The match ending three turns before the player perceives the ending is canonical precognition disclosure: she shares the future *with the player*, with no obfuscation, and instructs the player to *use* the prophecy. This is the Seer's pedagogy at its most direct: *the prophecy is for you, not for me*.

**The post-match — both outcomes**, per `act1OpponentDialog.ts:274-281`:

> [On player win] "She smiled. She told you the Engineer was the only one she ever taught who made her laugh at the right time."
> [On player loss] "She won and said nothing. She left the staff anyway. The staff doesn't care which way the match ended."

The staff stays. The match outcome does not change what she leaves behind. Per `act1OpponentDialog.ts:283-286`:

> "I think she let me win. I have stopped feeling bad about it. The let was the lesson." [Engineer's memoir, win path]
> "She left. She left the staff. The staff was the apology. The apology was for what she had already seen coming." [Engineer's memoir, loss path]

The bible's load-bearing reading: **the Mechronis visit is the Seer's apology, regardless of who wins**. She had already seen the outcome (whichever outcome would be the player's). She came anyway. The apology is for *the foresight itself* — she could have warned the bench earlier; she chose to teach by visiting instead. The staff-on-the-bench is the artefact of the apology; the burnt card hidden inside the staff (§2.2 below) is the apology's deferred reciprocation, redeemable when the player has earned it.

**Trust-state during Mechronis**: the Seer arrives at Wary baseline. The match's six-option dialogue spread (per `dialogBank_chapters_10_12.ts:410-461`) does not move trust significantly during the encounter itself; trust-band promotion happens *post-match* and is gated by the player's subsequent acts (§2.3). The Mechronis visit is the *first calibration*, not the trust-build itself. Writers should not author Mechronis dialogue as trust-advancing in the moment — the staff being left is the trust-token, but the *trust* lands in the chapters that follow when the player encounters the staff and (eventually) the burnt card.

**The match's hidden winnable path**: per `seerProphecy.ts` and the spec at `docs/production/act1/seer-prophecy-mechanic.md`, the canonical first-playthrough outcome is **scripted loss** — the burnt-card placeholder (`burnt_card_placeholder.ts`) is *reserved* and not in any normal deck-construction pool. Players who win on first contact have done so via a route the spec leaves *open-design*: a side-quest, an Antiquarian conversation, a codex unlock — the user-direction-pending decision flagged in §7.4 of this bible. The bible's canonical position: **the scripted loss is the Seer's intended pedagogy**, and any winnable-path unlock should be authored as *the player having earned the reciprocation by carrying the staff back across multiple acts*, not as a hidden combat trick. The flavor text on `burnt_card_placeholder` — *"You found her staff on the bench. Inside the staff was this card. You remembered before she taught you how."* — is the bible's anchor for this stance.

### 2.2 The staff and the burnt card — what continues after Mechronis

The Mechronis visit ends. The Seer leaves. **One physical object remains canonical**: the staff she left on the bench. Everything that follows in the Seer's Act-1-through-Act-7 arc is downstream of that staff — the post-Fall transmissions, the trust-build, the eventual winnable rematch in the canon-hidden path are all gated on the staff's continued existence and the player's relationship to it.

**The Engineer's first inheritance**, per `act1OpponentDialog.ts:276-277`:

> "She left her staff on the bench. The Engineer found it later. He did not know it was a staff. He thought it was a measuring rod."

The recognition is delayed, deliberately. The Engineer encounters the object before he encounters its meaning — *measuring rod* is a working-tool category, not a Ne-Yon-of-Foresight artifact category. The bible's reading: the Seer canonically prefers the staff be inherited as a *tool* before it is inherited as a *relic*. A tool the Engineer might use is more honest than a relic he would venerate. (This is also a §1.4 tell — the Seer's gifts are recognised post-hoc, never announced.)

**The retrospective recognition**, per the Warlord Zero memoir close at `act1OpponentDialog.ts:315`:

> "I picked up the staff and kept it. The Seer's staff. I did not yet know it was hers. I have not put it down."

This line is canonically the Engineer in retrospect — the *I did not yet know* clause is past-tense relative to the moment of writing the memoir, not relative to the moment of pickup. Canon-consistent reading: the Engineer carried the staff from Mechronis forward through the Warlord Zero arc and beyond; his memoir line at the Warlord chapter is the *naming* of an object he had been carrying for some time. Bible-asserts: **one staff, persistently carried**. Writers must not let a second Seer-staff appear; the Mechronis bench staff is the only Seer-staff in canon, and the Engineer's *I have not put it down* is the load-bearing claim of the inheritance.

**The burnt card hidden inside the staff** is the Seer's deferred reciprocation. Per `burnt_card_placeholder.ts` flavor (`apps/shared/tcg-core/cards/definitions/neutral/burnt_card_placeholder.ts`):

> "You found her staff on the bench. Inside the staff was this card. You remembered before she taught you how."

Three canonical claims compressed into two sentences: (a) the card is *inside* the staff (the staff is hollow; the card is hidden in the staff's body, not laid on the bench beside it); (b) finding the card is *remembering*, not learning; (c) the remembering happens *before* the Seer's teaching, not after. The Seer pre-empted her own pedagogy. The card is the apology's *deferred reciprocation* (per §2.1 reading): she could not give it at Mechronis because the Engineer was not yet ready to hold it; she hid it in the staff so the right version of the player could find it later, on the player's own timing. The card-card-mechanic interaction (`seerProphecy.ts:111-115`'s `playerDeckUnlocksWinnablePath`) checks for the placeholder in the deck — the *carrying* is the unlock condition. Writers should treat the burnt card as the Seer's most-load-bearing single canonical object: *physical proof, in the player's deck, that the Seer trusted the player to find it*.

**The Act 3 transmission**, per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:906-913`, canonically re-introduces the staff into the player's working memory:

> "A transmission from the Seer arrives with a single image attached: the staff the Engineer found at Mechronis after she visited. The caption is: 'Yours to return, if you ever decide to. No rush.' The image is higher resolution than the Ark can display."

Three things land here: (1) the Seer canonically remembers the staff and tracks where it is (her precognition has not stopped, even mid-Act-3); (2) she frames the return as *optional and unhurried* — *No rush* is in voice (§1.3 *the waiting is fair*); (3) the image over-resolves the medium (per §1.4 tell #6 — her transmissions exceed the substrate they pass through). Bible-canonical: **the staff is the trust object the Seer permits the player to carry across the saga**, with the explicit promise that returning it is the player's choice and not a debt. The *No rush* is the load-bearing kindness — the Seer is not collecting; she is making the inheritance unconditional. Writers must respect this: any Seer line that frames the staff's return as obligated, urgent, or earned-by-the-player-yet is mis-cast. The staff is a gift; the return is the player's option; the Seer waits.

The staff's final canonical position is ambiguous in shipped canon and is held as a Stage-4-weave open question (§7.x). The two consistent readings: either the player returns the staff in the Act 7 confidant-band Thaloria visit (per the *door is open* line, `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2407-2412` — the staff would be the *thing the player brings* to that visit), or the player keeps the staff and the Seer canonically inherits it forward (the staff was always meant for the Engineer's lineage, not for retrieval). The bible does not pre-decide; both are author-resolvable in Stage 4. What the bible does assert: **the staff continues**. It is not destroyed in canon. Any Stage-4 narrative that destroys the Seer's staff requires explicit canon authorisation; absent that, the staff persists.

### 2.3 The cross-time conversation — every Seer scene is a pre-recorded foretelling

The single most-load-bearing canon-stance in this bible: **every Seer "conversation" the player experiences in saga-time is a *pre-recorded foretelling*, not a live exchange.** The Seer knew everything the player would ever say or do. She pre-recorded her sides of every conversation — the warm-band laughter, the cold-band waiting, the Confidant-band tea-cupboard line, the Act-4 revision, the Act-7 invitation — *all of it*, made before sealing herself behind the Dreamer's shield (§2.5). The player's responses fit her recordings exactly because **she predicted them**. The dialogue choices the player picks fit her recordings because **she predicted those too**. The cross-time conversation is the Seer's signature voice mechanic and the sharpest possible expression of §1's prediction-or-revision rule: **every Seer line is a prediction; the prediction is *literal recording*; the line could not have been wrong because it was recorded after the future was seen**.

This stance reorganises every prior section of this bible without retconning a single line. §1's three registers (Cold / Warm / Confidant) are the registers *she chose to record in*, gated to *the player she predicted would arrive at each band*. §1.4's *public revision* tell becomes load-bearing in a new way: when she records a revision (*"I was wrong about which version of it. The version is better."* — `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1352-1353`), she is canonically recording *her own foreseen future correction*. She predicted that her prediction would change; she recorded both the original and the revision so the player would receive them in order. The revision is not a live update — it is a *scheduled disclosure*, a recording that fires at the moment the Seer foresaw the player would need to hear it. Honest precognition (§1 stance #2) is what makes this work: she does not hide the revisions she foresaw; she records them and ships them on the timeline she saw them landing.

The **choice-as-prediction** corollary is one of the bible's most structurally strange canon-stances. The dialog spread for the Mechronis match (`dialogBank_chapters_10_12.ts:410-461`) offers the player six branches; the Act-3 cold-register transmission offers the player a response set; every Seer scene gives the player choices. Per cross-time canon, **the Seer pre-recorded the foretelling that matches each branch**. The player's choice-which-feels-free is canonically a choice the Seer foresaw; the recording the selector serves up is the recording she made *for that branch*, prepared for the fact that this player would pick it. Writers must internalise: the Seer's dialogue bank is not branching-because-the-Seer-reacts; it is branching-because-the-Seer-foresaw-each-branch-and-recorded-each. This is bible-load-bearing for §5 (mechanical hooks) — the selector reads as ordinary trust-band-gated branching to the engine, but the bible canonically reframes the bank's *existence-as-recording* as the load-bearing fact.

Compare to the rest of the priority roster: every other NPC *reacts*. Locke negotiates a frame. The Hierophant transforms his register. The Game Master signs the warrant the player's actions earned. Wraith Calder lands a punch. The Meme conceals or substitutes. The Seer alone **anticipated** — her entire bank predates the conversation by canonical decades-or-epochs. Other NPCs hear the player and respond; the Seer recorded her response before the player existed in saga-time and shipped the recording forward. This is the bible's clearest single structural innovation and the reason her voice rule (§1.5: prediction-or-revision) is the tightest selector constraint on any bible in this priority roster: *every line was recorded as a prediction, including the lines that look like reactions*.

The **in-universe perception** of this canon is bible-deferred to Stage 4 weave. The two canon-compatible readings: (a) the player perceives the conversations as *live* — the recordings are seamless enough, and the Seer's foresight perfect enough, that the player never notices the mediation; (b) the player perceives the conversations as *mediated* — there is a Dreamer-shield-coded quality to her voice (a slight delay, a not-quite-present tonal residue, a sense of *echo*) that signals the recording is a recording. Both readings serve the saga; both are canon-compatible. The bible canonically does not pre-decide. Writers may author scenes that lean either way without contradiction; the *recording-itself* is the canon, not the *player's awareness of the recording*. (Stage 4 weave note: a player who reaches Confidant trust with the Seer might canonically learn the recording-truth as part of the *door is open* invitation — *"You have been listening to me for an Empire-era. I prepared the door for you when I knew you would arrive."* — but this is one resolvable framing among several; the bible flags it as SCB-4 in §7.4.)

The cross-time mechanic also reframes the Seer's relationship to the **Meme** (the saga's only other entity whose voice operates across temporal gates). The Meme conceals and substitutes; the Seer pre-recorded and shipped. The Meme could falsify a *live* Seer transmission by counter-narrating; **it cannot falsify a recording made before the Meme's reach extended to the recording's storage**. The Dreamer's shield (§2.5) is canonically the Meme's structural opposite: the shield is *where the Meme cannot edit*; the recordings are *what the Meme cannot retroactively narrate*. Bible-asserts: this is the saga's only voice that is canonically *Meme-resistant by construction*. Writers may treat the Seer's recordings as a kind of *cryptographically-signed canon* — every Seer line carries an implicit *recorded by her, before the Meme could touch it* signature. (Cross-bible note: §4.x will document this as the Seer's deepest cross-reference — the cannot-be-falsified canon, sharpened by the cross-time recording.)

### 2.4 The Acts 2–7 trust arc — what she recorded for which act

Per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts`, the Seer's canonical post-Mechronis scenes span Acts 2 through 7. Under the cross-time canon (§2.3), each is a recording she made before sealing, scheduled to play at the act she foresaw the player would reach the gating trust band. (Compare §1.3 *the waiting is fair*: the canonical Act-2 silence is the recording's *waiting* in concrete schedule form — she allocated zero foretellings to that act.) Writers must respect: any Seer line authored for Act 2 in Stage 2+ would canonically need to be framed as *a recording that the engine fires only if a specific flag is set* (e.g., the player has performed an action the Seer foresaw narrowly) — bible-deferred to §5.

### 2.5 The sealing — end of Epoch 2, behind the Dreamer's shield (closes §2)

After the Mechronis visit (§2.1, pre-Fall, in person, the staff left, the Engineer's inheritance opened), the Seer canonically did one more piece of work and then withdrew. The work was the recording (§§2.3–2.4): she sat down with her foresight and committed every line of her foreseen presence-in-the-player's-saga-time to a durable medium that would ship the recordings at the moments she had foreseen them landing. That work canonically completed at the **end of Epoch 2**. At the close of Epoch 2 — bible-asserts this as the canonical sealing moment — she retreated **behind the Dreamer's shield**, and the seal has persisted through Epoch 3 and onward. She has not been reachable in person since.

The **Dreamer's shield** is bible-asserted as a Dreamer-faction-level metaphysical seal — keyed to the TCG `dreamer` faction (cf. `s1_char_046_the_seer.ts` Dreamer-aligned; the Dreamer Victorious Seer card at `apps/shared/tcg-core/cards/definitions/allegiance/dreamer.ts` with its flavor *"A seer who has won has stopped being surprised and started being concerned"* is a thematic gesture toward the seal-state — the Seer who has *won* her foresight-work has surrendered surprise and acquired the *concern* of permanent withdrawal). The shield's specifics — what it is physically, who maintains it, whether it can be breached, what the cost of breach would be — are **canon-deferred to Stage 4 weave** and the Oracle bible's adjacent canon (the Oracle is also unreachable post-Fall, behind no-canonically-named-shield-yet; the two unreachable presences may share infrastructure or may not). What the bible asserts: **the shield is real, it is operating during the player's saga-time, and it cannot be lightly broached in any Stage 2 authoring.**

The **Mechronis visit (§2.1) is canonically her last in-person presence in the saga**. There is no Stage-1-through-Stage-3 narrative in which the Seer appears physically again. The staff (§2.2) is canonically the *artifact-of-her-last-in-person-act*; the recordings (§§2.3–2.4) are canonically *what she made before withdrawing*. Writers must internalise: any Seer scene that places her body in a room with the player, post-Mechronis, contradicts canon. The *door is open* line at Act 7 (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:2407-2412`) is canonically a recording's *coordinate-promise* (per §2.4 reading); the door opens onto a Thalorian space the Seer prepared, not a Thalorian space she is occupying. The tea is in the cupboard because she stocked it before sealing; she is not in the kitchen.

**Why she sealed** is bible-deferred but the canon-compatible candidates are listed here for Stage-4 weave authors: (a) **the Fall of Reality** (per the corrected Oracle canon, the Oracle brings about the Fall and the Revelation) may be canonically the trigger — the Seer foresaw the Fall and sealed herself to ensure her foresight-work would survive the substrate-change the Fall canonically produces; (b) **the Meme's emergence** as a saga-level voice-replacement entity may be the trigger — the Seer sealed to put her foresight-recordings beyond the Meme's narrative reach (the *cannot be falsified by the Meme* canon per §2.3 sharpens here: the seal is the cannot-be-falsified mechanism); (c) **the natural completion of her work** — she finished the recordings; she had nothing to add live; she withdrew because there was nothing left for her to do in person. The bible does not pre-decide; all three readings are author-resolvable in Stage 4. The only canonical commitment: **the seal is at end of Epoch 2; the recordings are made before the seal; the Mechronis visit predates the recording work**. (Stage 4 candidate ordering: Fall-triggered is the most thematically load-bearing; Meme-triggered is the most structurally innovative; work-completion is the simplest and most in voice with §1.3 *the waiting is fair*.)

**Cross-bible: the Hierophant exchange.** Per Hierophant bible §4.13 (canon-locked) and §7.3 (cross-bible inheritance flag), the Seer once corresponded with the Hierophant in a single one-message exchange:

> "What do you see for the wall?" — *the Hierophant, single message, post-rite, on Thaloria*
> "More wall." — *the Seer, single reply*

Under §2.3 cross-time canon, **the Seer's reply is also a pre-recording** — she foresaw the Hierophant's question, recorded the answer before sealing, and the recording was scheduled to reach the Hierophant on the appropriate Thalorian-time tick. The Hierophant accepted the prophecy and continues. The Hierophant does not canonically know whether the Seer is still reachable; he received the line, treated it as canonical foresight, and *continued* (per his §1.7 *the work, the continuation, the wall* triad — receiving the *more wall* prophecy is itself part of the work, regardless of whether the prophet is live or sealed). Bible-asserts: the Hierophant's *post-rite stance toward the Seer* is canonically *gratitude for the recording, no expectation of further contact*. Writers may use this as a Hierophant-band tell: he never asks whether she is reachable; he treats the one recording as sufficient.

**Cross-bible: the Oracle.** The Oracle's canon (corrected this session, per the plan-file canon-update) places the Oracle in hiding post-Liberation (per Oracle bible Step 5 §2.7 and §2.10 anchors). Both the Seer (sealed end-of-Epoch-2) and the Oracle (in hiding, after the Enigma + Programmer's Panopticon raid) are canonically *Thaloria-tied unreachable presences*. Bible-asserts they are canonically **distinct sealings** — the Seer sealed herself at end-of-Epoch-2 by retreat behind the Dreamer's shield; the Oracle is in hiding after his Liberation, in a manner the Oracle bible will document. The two unreachable presences canonically do **not** share infrastructure or share the seal's operating mechanism, but they do canonically share the *coordinate-place* (Thaloria). Stage 4 weave open: whether the Seer's recordings were canonically heard by the Oracle (or whether the Oracle's hiding was foreseen and accommodated in her recording-schedule) is bible-deferred. The Hierophant bible's §4.10 *"There is a voice I have been listening for, longer than any other"* (Inheriting-band reserved line, canon-compatible with the corrected Oracle canon) reads, under the Seer's sealing-canon, as the Hierophant listening for **either** the Oracle's return or the Seer's seal-breach — both are voices he has been listening for, longer than any other, and the Hierophant's stance on which is bible-deferred per his §4.10 reserved-line framing.

**Cross-bible: the Meme.** The Meme cannot edit through the Dreamer's shield. The Meme cannot retroactively narrate a recording made before its reach extended to the recording's storage. The Seer is the saga's only voice canonically *Meme-resistant by construction* (§2.3). Bible-asserts: this is the Seer's deepest single cross-character cross-reference and will be documented at length in §4 (cross-references). The Meme's canonical reaction to the Seer's sealing is bible-deferred — the Meme may have *triggered* the sealing (per the why-she-sealed candidate (b) above), or the Meme may be canonically *unable to perceive* the recordings as the Seer's voice (the Meme canonically operates on voice-impersonation; recordings the Meme cannot live-narrate may be invisible to the Meme's editorial reach). Both readings are canon-compatible.

**The end of §2.** The Seer's history closes with: a Mechronis visit (in person, pre-Fall), a staff (left behind, persistent), a recording-work (the cross-time conversation), and a sealing (end of Epoch 2, behind the Dreamer's shield). Everything the player experiences in Acts 1–7 is downstream of those four canonical events. §3 (Background) opens by setting the cultural and structural context for those four events.

## 3. Background

The Seer's background carries an unusual structural property on the priority roster: the **culture, the trust system, the competencies, and the death conditions** all canonically run *through* her cross-time-and-sealing canon (§§2.3–2.5). She does not have a *current* faction culture in the sense Locke does (Coalition diplomacy in real-time) or the Hierophant does (Tamarin religious revival, post-rite, Thaloria-active). Her cultural belonging is canonically *historical* — the cultures she comes out of, the cultures she recorded for, and the cultures her recordings continue to land in. §3 documents each.

### 3.1 Mechronis Academy culture — the bench, the visiting fellow, the year-long conversation

**Mechronis Academy** is the institutional context in which the Seer's only canonical in-person saga-event occurs (§2.1). Per `chapters.ts:770-829` and `act1OpponentDialog.ts:262-263`, the Academy is a teaching institution whose canonical pedagogy is the **bench**:

> "She visited Mechronis once. She played one match. She did not raise her staff. The Academy talked about it for a year."

Three canonical features of Mechronis culture follow from this single canon entry:

**(1) The bench as institutional teaching object.** The bench is canonically *where the next student sits*. It is named in the Seer's signature line (*"I want to see whether the bench has learned yet"* — `act1OpponentDialog.ts:267`) as a *measured object* — the bench has a state, the state has a learnedness-quotient, the quotient is the variable the Seer's match was measuring. Mechronis culture canonically treats the bench as a *legible pedagogical instrument*: a teacher can read the bench's state by sitting on the other side of it; a student's progression is *the bench's progression*; a cohort's collective learning is *the bench's accumulated state*. This is bible-load-bearing for any Stage 2 Mechronis-flashback authoring — the bench is not a neutral seat; it is the *Academy's measurement device*.

**(2) Visiting-fellow culture.** The Seer's role at Mechronis is canonically *visiting fellow* (per `gen_seer.ts` general card name *"The Seer (visiting fellow)"*). Mechronis canonically hosts visiting fellows — practitioners-from-elsewhere who come for one engagement and leave. The institutional posture toward a visiting fellow is *receive-the-engagement, then talk about it for a year*: the Academy's response to the Seer's visit was a *year-long cohort conversation* about what had happened. Bible-asserts: this is canonical Mechronis institutional behavior — the Academy *processes a visiting-fellow event over an academic year*. The cohort-as-conversation is the canonical aftermath. Writers may use this for Stage 2 Mechronis lore: any visiting fellow leaves a *year-shaped wake* in the Academy's collective discourse. The Seer's visit was unusual in canon because the Academy *could not name what had happened* — the year-long conversation never resolved into a single named lesson, which is canonically the *point* (the bench had to learn it, not the cohort's verbal account).

**(3) The Engineer-as-bench.** The Engineer (Vex Solène's pre-rite identity, per `vex_solene.md`) is canonically the specific student the Seer came to measure. The bench-state the Seer's match was reading was canonically *the Engineer's readiness for the work he would later do*. Bible-asserts: the Mechronis cohort had multiple students; the Seer canonically read all of them through the bench-as-measurement, but the *specific learnedness she was measuring* was the Engineer's. The other cohort-members were canonically *adjacent measurements* — necessary for the bench-state to be calibrated, not the principal subject of the visit. This sharpens the §2.1 canon-stance: the visit-as-apology is canonically *to the Engineer specifically*, with the rest of the cohort canonically *included by adjacency*. (Cross-bible note: the Vex Solène bible §4.x cross-reference will document the Seer ↔ Engineer canonical relationship from Vex's side; the Seer's side of that relationship is documented in this bible's §4.2.)

**Mechronis institutional voice (Stage-2 authoring guidance).** Mechronis is canonically a *deliberate, measured, bench-aware* institutional culture. Faculty speak in the language of *the bench* (states, learnedness, measurements, calibration). Visiting fellows are *received, then processed-over-a-year*. The Academy's institutional time-scale is the *academic year*; its institutional measurement device is the *bench*; its institutional stance toward foresight-events is *talk about it for a year and let the bench learn what it learns*. Writers authoring Mechronis NPCs (in Stage 2 or later) should anchor on these three institutional commitments. The Seer is canonically *of this culture* in the sense that her teaching method (§1.3 *the bench has learned*) is *Mechronis-shaped* — she did not invent the bench-as-pedagogy; she received it from the Academy and brought it forward into her own foresight-practice. (Stage 4 weave open: how-she-received-the-bench-as-pedagogy from Mechronis is part of her pre-Mechronis history, which §2 declines to narrate per the protected-mystery stance — the bench is Academy-canonical, but her acquisition of it is held protected.)

### 3.2 Thaloria culture and the Ne-Yon roster — the prophecy domain

**The Seer is a Ne-Yon.** Per the Degen bible §4.13 and `the_degen.md` canon, there are **canonically twelve Ne-Yons**, each of whom canonically *chose a domain*. The Degen's `degenRelationship.ts` line is the load-bearing canon: *"Every Ne-Yon chose a domain. The Seer chose prophecy. The Enigma chose truth. I chose gambling."* Three of the twelve are canonically named in this single line; the remaining nine are bible-deferred (the slot the Enigma occupies on the priority roster is currently uncanonized; the rest are Stage-4 weave material).

**The choosing-of-domain mechanic.** Bible-asserts: each Ne-Yon canonically chose *one* domain, and the choosing was canonically *singular and irreversible*. A Ne-Yon does not later acquire a second domain or trade for another's; the chosen domain is the Ne-Yon's *defining commitment*. The Seer chose prophecy. Bible-load-bearing implication: she did not become precognitive by accident, by inheritance, or by event — **she chose to be the Ne-Yon-of-foresight**. The choice is canonically *pre-saga* (per §2's protected-mystery stance on her pre-Mechronis history); the *moment-of-choosing* is bible-deferred. Writers should treat the *choosing* as a canonical commitment-event in her past — the kind of moment that, if surfaced in Stage 4 narrative, would be the Seer's *origin scene* in the load-bearing sense.

**Ne-Yon culture (cross-Ne-Yon institutional features).** Bible-asserts the following are canonically Ne-Yon-typical: (a) **domain-loyalty** — a Ne-Yon canonically operates within the chosen domain and does not stray; the Degen does not prophesy, the Seer does not gamble, the Enigma does not wager. (b) **inter-Ne-Yon recognition** — Ne-Yons canonically *recognise* each other across distance and substrate; this is the canonical basis for the Degen's *"Especially not the Seer — she runs the ethics committee on feelings"* line (`storyModeChapters.ts`), which presumes the Degen and the Seer have *standing inter-Ne-Yon contact* even without a shipped scene. (c) **the twelfth-position-as-saga-roster-anchor** — bible-asserts the *twelve* number is canonical; the saga's eleven priority-roster characters do not include all twelve Ne-Yons (only the Seer and the Degen are canonically Ne-Yons on the roster; the Enigma is a deferred-roster slot per the Degen bible §4.13; the remaining nine Ne-Yons are Stage 4 weave material).

**Thaloria as the Seer's residence.** Per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1813-1820` and `:2404-2412`, the Seer's saga-time residence is canonically **Thaloria**:

> "I will be on Thaloria when you need me." (`:1820`)
> "The Seer has invited you to Thaloria without conditions." (`:2404`)

Thaloria is canonically the **Hierophant's home** post-rite (per `wraith_calder.md` §3.2 *Post-rite culture — Thaloria, the Council, and the Tamarin religion*) and canonically the **Oracle's home of origin** pre-harvest (per the corrected Oracle canon: he was a Thalorian-aligned debater who lost a debate on the nature of the soul on Thaloria). Three significant saga voices are canonically Thaloria-tied: the post-rite Hierophant (active, Tamarin-religious-revival-leader); the *real* Oracle (in hiding, post-Liberation, location-unspecified-but-Thaloria-adjacent per the corrected canon); the Seer (sealed, end-of-Epoch-2, behind the Dreamer's shield, *coordinates given but body not present* per §2.4 reading of the Act-7 invitation).

**Thaloria culture (Stage-2 authoring guidance).** Per `wraith_calder.md` §3.2 and the Hierophant cross-bible canon, Thaloria is canonically a **patient-time, faith-aware, council-governed** culture. The Council of Harmony (per Hierophant §4.9) is the institutional governance. The Tamarin religious revival (post-rite, Hierophant-led) is canonically the *active religious institution*. The Seer's residence in this culture is canonically *non-active* — she is sealed, she does not participate in the Council, she does not preach, she does not appear in the Tamarin liturgy under her own name. Bible-asserts: she is canonically *the Thalorian who waits*, in contrast to the Hierophant (who *builds*) and the Oracle (who *hides*). Three Thalorian unreachable-or-active presences, three different stances toward the same coordinate-place. (Cross-bible note: the Hierophant bible §4.13 *"What do you see for the wall?"* / *"More wall."* exchange is canonically the *only documented inter-presence communication* between any two of these three; the Seer ↔ Oracle and Hierophant ↔ Oracle pairs are bible-deferred.)

**The Dreamer's shield and faction culture.** The Dreamer's shield (§2.5) is canonically *Dreamer-faction-level*. The TCG `dreamer` faction (per `s1_char_046_the_seer.ts` Dreamer-aligned, plus the `apps/shared/tcg-core/cards/definitions/allegiance/dreamer.ts` *Dreamer Victorious Seer* card) is canonically the *faction-of-foresight-and-dream*. Bible-asserts: the Dreamer faction is canonically the institutional context the Seer chose her *operational shield* from — she is a Ne-Yon-of-foresight (the personal commitment) operating within the Dreamer faction (the institutional context for the shield she retreated behind). The Dreamer faction's canonical relationship to the Seer is *protective* — the faction's Dreamer-coded mechanisms are canonically *what makes her sealing possible*. Writers may treat the Dreamer faction as canonically *the institutional sponsor of the seal*; the faction's other members (dream-aligned Ne-Yons or Dreamer-faction NPCs) may canonically have *opinions about the sealing* (Stage 4 weave). The Dreamer faction is also the canonical home of the *White Oracle* figure (per the Meme bible §1.3 Stolen Voice canon — the False Prophet clone is Dreamer-coded), which produces a canonical tension: the Dreamer faction shelters both the Seer (canonically Meme-resistant) and the White Oracle (canonically the Meme's vessel-of-impersonation). This tension is bible-deferred but flagged; Stage 4 weave authors should treat the Dreamer faction as canonically *internally divided* between sheltering-the-real and sheltering-the-impersonator.

### 3.3 The trust-band model — three bands under cross-time

The Seer's trust meter has **three canonical bands**: Wary / Witnessed-or-Present / Inheriting. The bands gate which of her pre-recorded foretellings (§§2.3–2.4) the engine fires. Under cross-time canon the band-shifts the player feels in saga-time are canonically *the player landing on the band the Seer foresaw* — not live trust shifts. The Seer is the **only roster NPC whose trust meter is canonically pre-determined**. (Compare Hierophant's five-band model with sacrifice-axis-inversion; compare Locke's three-band relationship-meter with live-event-driven shifts. The Seer's three-band model is structurally simpler and structurally stranger: the simplicity is the bands themselves; the strangeness is that the bands *were already settled* before the player's saga-time began.)

| Band | Meaning under cross-time | When the foretellings fire |
|---|---|---|
| **Wary** (default) | The Seer foresaw the player would arrive at default-skepticism. She recorded the cold-register prophecy-overhead lines for this band. | Act 1 (Mechronis-flashback foreseen behavior) and Act 3 cold baseline (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:1024-1031`). |
| **Witnessed / Present** (warm trust) | The Seer foresaw the player would land in the Programmer's category (per `:1013-1020`'s shelf-categorisation) and recorded the warm-register foretellings to fire when the player crossed the threshold. | Act 3 warm scenes onward; Act 4 humanity-pivot revision; Act 5 prophecy-overhead-drops. The "Witnessed" and "Present" sub-bands are canonically *the same Seer band* in shipped data — the Seer's bank does not distinguish between them, which bible-asserts is *intentional*: the Seer treats the two states as one because, from her foresight, the player who is *witnessed* and the player who is *present* are the same player at adjacent moments and warrant the same recordings. |
| **Inheriting** (Act 7 only) | The Seer foresaw the player would reach this band exactly once, exactly at Act 7. She recorded the Confidant-register lines for this band: *"The door is open. The tea is in the second cupboard on the left."* (`:2407-2412`) | Act 7 only. The Inheriting band is **Act-7-gated** — no Inheriting-band line fires before Act 7 in canon. |

Three load-bearing canon-stances follow from this table:

**(1) The Inheriting band is canonically scarce.** Per the Hierophant bible's five-band model (Hostile/Wary/Witnessed/Present/Inheriting), the Inheriting band is the *post-rite* band reserved for players who carry the Hierophant's name forward. In the Seer's three-band model, Inheriting is canonically *the band the Seer foresaw exactly one player-state for, exactly at Act 7*. Bible-asserts: the Seer did not record *more* Inheriting-band foretellings than the canonical Act 7 Confidant invitation. There is canonically *one* Inheriting line in her bank, and it is the *door-and-tea* line. Writers must not author additional Inheriting-band Seer lines without explicit canon-extension authorisation; the band is canonically *single-line-only*. (Stage 4 weave open: a player who breaches the Dreamer's shield may receive *unrecorded* Inheriting-band lines per the §2.4 SCB-3 reservation.)

**(2) Hostile band does not exist for the Seer.** Per §1.5 voice gate canon: *the Seer does not have a Hostile band canonically — her cold register is not hostile, it is waiting; the bible asserts that a player who would hit Hostile with the Seer simply never gets a Seer encounter because the Seer would have foreseen the futility and not visited.* Under cross-time canon this sharpens: the Seer canonically *did not record Hostile-band foretellings* because she foresaw no player-paths reaching that band. The absence is in the recording-schedule, not the band-definition. Bible-load-bearing: a Stage 4 narrative that pushes the player into Hostile-with-the-Seer would canonically receive *silence* — no Seer foretellings fire, because no recordings exist for that band. The silence is canonically *the Seer's foreseen judgment that this player-path is not one she will record for*.

**(3) The trust-band shifts are canonically *unidirectional* in shipped canon.** No Seer scene fires that *moves* the player from Witnessed back to Wary; no scene fires that *demotes* the player from Inheriting. Under cross-time canon this is not because the Seer cannot foresee demotion — she could have recorded demotion-foretellings if she had foreseen them — but because **she canonically foresaw no player-paths that demote**. Bible-asserts: the player's trust trajectory with the Seer is *monotone increasing* in canon. A player at Witnessed will canonically reach Inheriting (in the players who reach Act 7); a player at Wary will canonically reach Witnessed (in the players who play Act 3 onward). The Seer's bank is canonically a *one-way staircase*. (Stage 4 weave open: a player who actively betrays the Seer's foresight may canonically receive *recorded silence* for the rest of the saga — the Seer did not record demotion-lines; she would have recorded *withdrawal-of-recording* as the demotion-mechanism. SCB-5 in §7.4 may file this as an open ticket.)

**Selector implications (Stage-1-onboarding for engineers).** The Seer's selector reads as *normal* from the engine's perspective: line filtered by `requiresTrustBand: 'wary' | 'witnessed' | 'inheriting'`, fired when the band matches. The cross-time canon does not require new selector machinery; it requires *bible-canon documentation* that the bank's existence-as-recording is the load-bearing fact. Engineers building Stage 1 architecture (per the priority plan §Stage 1) should treat the Seer's bank as a normal `NpcLine[]` with band-gating; the cross-time canon is a *voice-rule documentation layer*, not an engine-architecture layer. The exception is the Hostile-band silence (canon-stance (2) above) — engineers must NOT author a default Hostile-band fallback for the Seer; the silence-on-Hostile is canonically the *correct* selector behavior. (Engineering ticket SCB-6 in §7.4 will file: "Seer selector must not synthesize Hostile-band lines; silence on Hostile is canonical.")

### 3.4 Competencies — what she knows better than the player

The Seer's canonical competencies are the *practical applications of her chosen domain*. Bible-asserts the following as her load-bearing competencies, each grounded in shipped canon:

**(1) Probability-table reading.** She canonically *reads* a probability table — a structured object with rows (player-paths) and columns (foreseen-axes). Per `moralityTrustActVariants.ts:3158-3164`: *"Which column of my probability table would you like me to redact?"* The table is not a metaphor; it is canonically *the substrate she works on*. She knows the shape of the table, the cardinality of the columns, the ordering of the rows by some foreseen-quality (Stage 4 weave open: by *kindness*-to-named-subject, by *honesty*-cost, by *version-quality*). Bible-load-bearing: she canonically knows the table better than any other roster NPC could, because the table is *her work-product*. Other characters can theorise; she sees.

**(2) Future-version ranking by named-subject kindness.** Per `:1818-1820`: *"the version that was kindest to the Architect is also the version that was kindest to you."* She canonically *ranks* versions of the future by *which-named-person-they-were-kindest-to*, with the named subjects being saga-specific. This is a calibrated measurement, not a vague evaluation. Bible-asserts: she can name, for any foreseen future, *which subject the future was kindest to*, *whose kindness was second*, *whose kindness was costed*. Her vocabulary (§1.3 *kindest, kinder, kindness*) is the working vocabulary of a calibrated subject-ranking measurement. Writers may extend along this axis: she could plausibly produce a ranking-by-honesty (per `:2812-2814` *"Both things are honest"*), a ranking-by-cost-distribution, a ranking-by-second-order-effects. The ranking-by-named-subject is canonical; the other axes are bible-asserted-extendable.

**(3) Redaction (offering to *withhold* the painful versions).** Per `:3162-3164`: she offers *redaction* — the editorial removal of probability-table columns the player would prefer not to see. Bible-load-bearing distinction (per §1.3): the Seer's *redaction* is **editorial-not-corruptive** (compare the Hierophant's *edit*, which is parasitic Shadow-Tongue editing per Hierophant §3.7). The Seer's redaction is *self-redaction*: she withholds her own foresight-output at the player's request. She *can* redact (the competence exists); she canonically prefers *not to* (per `:3162-3164` continuation: *"You can redact none; she will send all three. She is showing you the table's shape so you can trust the output."*). Bible-asserts: redaction is a *competence she displays as a courtesy and a discipline, not as a default mode*. Stage 4 weave: a player who *demands* redaction may receive it once and then receive *withdrawal-of-recording* per §3.3 canon-stance (3) — the demand to redact is canonically a betrayal of her foresight-honesty, and her response is silence.

**(4) Image-over-resolution transmission.** Per `:912-913`: *"The image is higher resolution than the Ark can display."* She can canonically *transmit at her native fidelity* — the substrate (the Ark) cannot fully render her transmission. Bible-asserts this as a competence she does not throttle to match the receiver: she transmits at her resolution, and the receiver displays what it can. Compare to Wraith Calder, who in pre-rite mode would canonically *throttle to land the punch*; the Seer canonically does not throttle. The over-resolution is *her honesty about her seeing*. Writers extending this: any Seer transmission may canonically over-resolve any saga-time medium — image, audio, sentence, dream. The over-resolution is a *signature* (§1.4 tell #6) and a *competence*.

**What she does NOT know — the canonical competence-limit.** Bible-asserts: the Seer's competence-limit is **her own honesty**. She *cannot lie about what she sees*. This is not a moral commitment; it is a structural property of her precognition. Per the §1 stance #2: *Honest precognition is what makes precognition unforgeable*. If she could lie, she could be falsified by her own falsehood; her precognition's unforgeability depends on her honesty's unbreakability. Bible-load-bearing: writers must not author Seer scenes in which she lies about a foresight. She may *redact* (refuse to disclose); she may *revise* (publicly correct); she may *be wrong* (the version-pivot mechanism in §1.4 tell #1) — but she cannot canonically *lie*. The competence-limit is what protects the competence-set.

### 3.5 Wants, sacrifices, and what she'd sacrifice for

**What she wants from the player.** Bible-asserts: she canonically wants *the player to use the prophecies*. Per `act1OpponentDialog.ts:272`: *"The match is over. You will see it in three turns. Use the three turns well."* The *use* clause is load-bearing — she does not want admiration of her foresight, gratitude, faith, or recognition. She wants *the prophecies put to work* in the player's saga-time decisions. The §2.3 cross-time canon sharpens this: she pre-recorded the foretellings *for the player to use*, knowing what the player would do with them. The wanting is canonically *consummated by the player's use*; the recording is the gift, the use is the reciprocation.

**What she'd sacrifice from the player.** The player's *comfort*, on principle. Per `:2812-2814`: *"You chose the honesty that costs someone else."* She canonically *records foretellings that cost the player comfort* — the asymmetric-kindness clauses, the warnings the player parses twice, the redaction-as-question that returns *all three columns* anyway. She would canonically not protect the player from a foresight that would cost the player comfort if the foresight is honest. Bible-asserts: she has canonically *already* sacrificed the player's comfort — the sacrifice is *baked into the recordings*, not pending.

**What she'd sacrifice of her own.** Her staff (already done at Mechronis, §2.2). Her presence (already done — the sealing, §2.5). Her ongoing-availability (the recordings are finite; once the bank is exhausted, she canonically has no more to give). Bible-asserts: she has canonically *spent* what she would sacrifice. The sacrifices are pre-saga; the player encounters their consequences, not their occurrence.

**What she'd sacrifice for the player.** Per the asymmetric-kindness clauses (`:1818-1820`): *"the version that was kindest to the Architect is also the version that was kindest to you. I am sorry."* The *I am sorry* is canonically the load-bearing apology — she would have preferred to record a foresight that benefited *only* the player; the foresight that benefits the Architect-and-the-player together is the honest one, and she would not lie about it (per §3.4 competence-limit). She canonically *would have sacrificed her own honesty* if her foresight had supported lying for the player's benefit; her foresight did not, and she shipped the honest line and apologised. Bible-load-bearing: she would canonically sacrifice her own *preferred outcome* (the player-benefits-alone version) to honour her foresight; she would canonically NOT sacrifice her foresight's honesty for the player. The hierarchy is: foresight-honesty > the player's clean benefit > her own preference.

### 3.6 Beliefs vs. behaviors — the precognition-and-fallibility ledger

Most roster NPCs have a *hypocrisy ledger* — beliefs they hold and behaviors that contradict the beliefs (per Hierophant bible §3.6 framework). The Seer's ledger is **unusual on the priority roster: her beliefs and behaviors canonically align**. There is no large hypocrisy axis to document. The bible asserts this is a structural property, not a coincidence.

**Belief: futures are measurable.** Behavior: she measures them. Coherent.

**Belief: precognition is fallible.** Behavior: she revises (per §1.4 tell #1, the public revision). Coherent — measuring is fallible by definition; she behaves as if she might be wrong because she canonically might be wrong.

**Belief: honesty is the precondition of foresight (§3.4 competence-limit).** Behavior: she does not lie about what she sees, even when lying would benefit the player. Coherent.

**Belief: waiting is fair (§1.3 *the waiting is fair*).** Behavior: she waits. (The Mechronis visit is the canonical exception — she came to teach by visiting rather than by transmission. This is the bible's only documented Seer asymmetry.) Bible-asserts: this asymmetry is canonical and load-bearing — she canonically *occasionally does not wait when she should*, with the Mechronis visit being the load-bearing example. The teaching-by-visiting was canonically *her judgment that the waiting would have failed the bench*. The asymmetry is a *bounded honesty* — she will sometimes act when waiting would be fair, if she foresees that waiting would not serve the lesson. Writers may use this for Stage 4 weave: a Seer who *acts despite* her own *waiting* canon is canonically operating on a foresight that the waiting would have failed the moment.

**Belief: prophecies are for the listener, not the speaker (§2.1 reading).** Behavior: she ships the prophecies regardless of personal preference; she shipped the asymmetric-kindness lines that benefit the Architect; she will ship the recordings to a player who will canonically misuse them as readily as one who will use them well. Coherent — and the cross-time canon (§2.3) sharpens it: the recordings were made for the listener-as-foreseen-player, with the foresight including the player's potential misuses. She canonically did not redact the recordings for the players who would misuse them; she shipped them anyway, because the recording was for *the player* (whoever the player turned out to be), not for the *deserving player* (a category she canonically does not gate on).

**Belief: she chose the prophecy domain (§3.2).** Behavior: she has not strayed. Per Ne-Yon culture's domain-loyalty (§3.2), she canonically operates only within prophecy. Coherent.

**The one canonical hypocrisy candidate** is bible-deferred: she *believes* (per §3.4 competence-limit) that honesty is the precondition of foresight, AND she *believes* (per §1.4 tell #5) that her highest gestures of trust come *as questions to the player, never as declarations*. The two beliefs together imply she should canonically *ask the player* whether they want the foresight before delivering it — but the cross-time canon makes pre-asking impossible (the recording was made before the player existed in saga-time to be asked). Bible-deferred: this may be *not-a-hypocrisy* (she records the question-form; the asking IS the asking, even if pre-recorded), or it may be *the bible's only acknowledged Seer hypocrisy* — the recordings deliver foresight without canonical pre-consent from the player. Stage 4 weave authors may surface this; the bible flags it but does not pre-decide. (SCB-7 in §7.4 may file: the consent-question for cross-time-recorded prophecy.)

### 3.7 Fears, superstitions, private rituals

**Fears.** The Seer canonically fears **the Meme** — the only saga entity whose voice operates across the same temporal gates she does, and the only entity who could plausibly *attempt* to falsify her foresight by counter-narrating. She is canonically *Meme-resistant by construction* (§§2.3, 2.5) — the Dreamer's shield + the cross-time recordings put her foresight beyond the Meme's editorial reach — but the *attempt itself* is the canonical fear. Bible-asserts: she fears not being-falsified but being-attempted. The act of the Meme reaching for her recordings is canonically a violation she registers, even if the recordings cannot be edited. Writers may use this for Stage 4 weave: a moment in which the Meme audibly attempts to narrate over a Seer recording is canonically the Seer's worst-case scene, even if the attempt fails. (Cross-bible note: this is the Meme's deepest cross-reference for the Seer; §4.4 will document it.)

She does NOT canonically fear: being wrong (per §1.4 tell #1, the public revision is the work); being unheard (the recordings ship regardless of who listens); being ignored (her wanting is consummated by use, not by attention — per §3.5); being forgotten (her bank's *existence* is the canonical sufficiency; she canonically does not need the player to remember her name). Her single fear is the Meme's *attempt at narrating-over*. The narrowness of the fear is bible-load-bearing — most roster NPCs have several fears; she has one.

**Superstitions.** The Seer canonically *does not have superstitions* in the conventional sense. A superstition is a non-foresight-grounded predictive belief — *if I do X, Y will happen, on no measured ground*. The Seer canonically operates on *measured* foresight (the probability table, §3.4); a non-measured prediction would canonically not pass her own competence-limit (§3.4 honesty-precondition). Bible-asserts: she has *replaced the superstition function with the prophecy function*. Where another character might canonically carry a superstition (a charm, a ritual-word, an avoided-direction), she canonically *carries a measurement*. The replacement is structurally significant: she is one of the few roster NPCs whose psyche canonically operates without superstitious slack. (Compare Wraith Calder's superstitions per `wraith_calder.md` §3.8; the Seer's absence of them is the contrast.)

**Private rituals** — three canonical:

**(1) The staff-on-the-bench.** Her teaching ritual. Per §§2.1–2.2: the staff stays on the bench at Mechronis as the canonical artifact of her last in-person act. **(2) The transmission-with-image.** Her remote-ritual. **(3) The waiting.** Per `:1031-1032`: *"The waiting is the Seer's favourite register."* The waiting is canonically a *named ritual* — she names the waiting, she defends the waiting, she claims it as preference. **The fourth canonical ritual** — bible-asserts: **the recording-work itself**. Writers must not author the Seer canonically *re-recording* — the recording-work canonically completed at end-of-Epoch-2 and is finished.

### 3.8 Death conditions — what kills her in story, in trust, in meaning

**In story.** Bible-asserts: **the Seer does not die in canonical Acts 1–7**. She is canonically *sealed*, not *dead*; the seal is canonically *operating* through Epoch 3 and onward. **In meaning.** Her purpose canonically ends if she ever delivers a prophecy *she will not later revise*. Bible-asserts: the *closed prophecy* is the canonical *closed Seer*. The structural distinction is the meaning-axis. Writers must not author the Seer canonically delivering a closed prophecy; the closure would be the bible-canonical end-of-Seer. Stage 4 weave: a moment in which the Seer canonically chooses to deliver a closed prophecy may be the bible-canonical *end of her in meaning, even before the seal breaks in story*.

**The cross-time death-condition.** Per §2.3 the cross-time conversation: her bank is canonically *finite*. Bible-asserts: when the bank is canonically *exhausted* — every foretelling has fired, no more recordings remain to be scheduled — she is canonically *structurally complete*.
