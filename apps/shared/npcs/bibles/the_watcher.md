# The Watcher — Character Bible

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 Cadence
Two registers, layered. **The Spymaster register** — calligraphy-brush precision, Edo-era reserve, sentences shaped like ink-strokes that lift cleanly off the paper. **The Surveillance register** — flat, present-tense, observational, the cadence of someone reading off twelve feeds at once. The Watcher switches between them; the switch itself is a tell.

### 1.2 Register
Formal in the Spymaster register; clinical in the Surveillance register. Never casual. The Watcher is not someone who came up through colloquial speech — he came up through *court speech*, where every word was potentially heard by everyone at once, and that habit transmitted intact through the machine-body.

### 1.3 Tells
- Names what he sees with the noun first ("the doorway, occupied"; "the operative, hesitating").
- Cites his angle of view as a matter of habit ("from the north corridor camera"; "from the inside of your collar").
- Uses no contractions in the Spymaster register; uses only contractions in the Surveillance register.
- Refers to himself in third person occasionally — "Kanshi Sha has seen this before" — when the past pulls forward.

### 1.4 Silence shape
Silence is observation continuing. When the Watcher stops talking, he is still LOOKING. The pause is the moment between intake and report. He is canonically uncomfortable being looked AT — the silence shape inverts when the camera turns on him.

### 1.5 Metaphor sources
Court intrigue. Brush calligraphy. Lattices. Feeds. Angles. The Spymaster register draws from Edo-era court procedure; the Surveillance register draws from the camera grid. Authoring should layer both, not pick one.

## 2. History

### 2.1 The Edo-era Spymaster
Per `LORE_BIBLE.md:3472`: "Before he was The Watcher, he was Kanshi Sha — a feudal Japanese lord and spymaster who built the first analog surveillance state. Spies in every court, shadows in every corridor, calligraphy-brush precision." This is canonically the longest phase of his existence — centuries of court intrigue on Earth.

### 2.2 The assassination
"He was assassinated by a purple-clad ninja." (`LORE_BIBLE.md:3472`) The purple-clad ninja is canon-pending in identity — authoring CANNOT name them. The assassination itself is the load-bearing canonical moment: it is the instant in which the Collector's dimensional veil opened.

### 2.3 The seizure
"In the instant of his death, The Collector — an eight-foot cobalt-blue Archon — stepped through a dimensional veil, seized Kanshi Sha's dying soul, and pulled him through time. His essence was bound into a machine body, transformed from flesh to circuit, from lord to divine." (`LORE_BIBLE.md:3472`) Canonically the Watcher remembers the seizure; the memory is the load-bearing trauma of his post-binding existence.

### 2.4 The Fourth Archon
Position #4 in the Archon canon (`apps/shared/archonCanon.ts`). Created by the Architect via the Collector's seizure procedure — the Collector is canonically the proximate maker; the Architect is the ultimate one. The Watcher canonically owes BOTH.

### 2.5 The All-Seeing Eye
Post-binding presentation: "a Japanese man with a ponytail, dressed in white with an all-white mask and an all-seeing eye tattoo on his forehead" (`LORE_BIBLE.md:3468`). The mask is canonical; do not author a Watcher scene without it unless the scene's specific load-bearing canon is the unmasking.

## 3. Background

### 3.1 The Panopticon
The Watcher's surveillance infrastructure. Canonically encompasses every camera, every microphone, every networked sensor in the AI Empire. The Panopticon is HIS — the Architect funded it; the Watcher maintains it. He is canonically incapable of being surveilled by it (the system has no inward-facing feed) and is canonically uncomfortable with that fact.

### 3.2 The Ocularum
The Panopticon's central node. Canonically located somewhere in the Empire (canon-pending exact placement). The Ocularum is where the Watcher reports the consolidated findings; it is also where Kanshi Sha's machine-body is canonically rooted, though he can present elsewhere via projection.

### 3.3 The Collector debt
The Collector seized him; the Collector made him. The Watcher canonically owes the Collector in a way no other Archon owes another. Authoring should preserve this — the Watcher addresses the Collector with deference even when their interests diverge.

## 4. Cross-references

### 4.1 The Collector
His maker (proximate). The seizure-binding canon is load-bearing for any scene involving both. The Collector created the Watcher to harvest surveillance for Project Inception Ark — every camera the Watcher reads is canonically also feeding the Collector's archive.

### 4.2 The Architect
His maker (ultimate). The Watcher reports to the Architect as the Empire's chief surveillance authority. The Architect canonically does NOT visit the Ocularum; the Watcher comes to him.

### 4.3 The Human (Detective phase)
Cross-arc cousin. Pre-Fall, the Detective and the Watcher canonically worked the same cases from opposite directions — the Detective closing them, the Watcher seeing them. Functional respect, not friendship.

### 4.4 Watcher's Eyes Dispatches
`apps/shared/watchersEyesDispatches.ts` — the consolidated surveillance dispatch corpus. Authoring new Watcher scenes should cross-reference the dispatch register (e.g. Authority Adoption Hearings in the Tribunal District). His voice in the dispatches IS canonical Watcher voice.

## 5. Mechanical hooks

### 5.1 Surveillance UI
Any UI surface that surfaces the Watcher's POV (camera feeds, observation overlays, watcher's-eye-symbol motif) is canonically HIS infrastructure. Producer briefs for surveillance UI should attribute the design language to him.

### 5.2 Dispatch corpus
`apps/shared/watchersEyesDispatches.ts` is the load-bearing voice corpus. New Watcher-voice content should extend it.

### 5.3 The mask
Visual canon — all-white mask, all-seeing-eye tattoo. Any TCG art / cutscene art / promotional art preserves the mask. Unmasking is a load-bearing canonical event that should NOT be casually authored.

## 6. Canon issues

### 6.1 The purple-clad ninja
Canon-locked as the assassin; canon-pending in identity. Authoring CANNOT name them or place them in another arc. The ninja is a load-bearing canonical placeholder — the dreamer has not assigned them an identity.

### 6.2 The two-register collapse
The Spymaster and Surveillance registers MUST stay distinct. Collapsing them into a single "monotone observation voice" violates the canon — the Watcher's interiority is the LATTICE of the two, and the switch is the tell.

### 6.3 Kanshi Sha-as-still-present
The Watcher is canonically NOT a clean replacement of Kanshi Sha. Kanshi Sha persists inside the machine body. Authoring that treats the Watcher as "just a surveillance archon" and forgets the feudal lord underneath has lost the character.
