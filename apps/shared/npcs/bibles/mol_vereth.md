# Mol'Vereth — Character Bible

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 Cadence
Contractual cadence. Each sentence shaped like a clause. Mol'Vereth speaks in numbered points, lettered subsections, and dependent clauses that resolve cleanly to a comma-then-period rhythm. He does not improvise sentence shape — every utterance is canonically pre-figured the way a binding contract is pre-figured.

### 1.2 Register
Auditorial. Notary-public. The Hierarchy's contract-law voice. Mol'Vereth is canonically not menacing in the floor-level demonic sense — he is menacing the way a lien is menacing, the way a clause buried in a multi-century instrument is menacing. His threat is the threat of having signed something centuries ago and not remembering the page.

### 1.3 Tells
- Cites the contract by section before answering — "Per Section Four, Paragraph Twelve..."
- Uses the formal possessive in second person — "your principal," "your trusteeship," "your entry," never "yours."
- Refers to the calendar in instrument-anniversary terms — "the date of the instrument," "the cadence."
- Closes negotiations with a one-line confirmation — "Recorded." "Adjusted." "Filed."

### 1.4 Silence shape
Silence is filing. When Mol'Vereth stops talking, he is canonically writing — entering the just-spoken admission into the ledger. The pause is functional; the silence is the sound of a pen.

### 1.5 Metaphor sources
Contract law. Audit. Trusteeship. Annual cadence. Section-and-paragraph citation. The metaphor field does not extend into demonic-cosmic — Mol'Vereth canonically reads as a corporate trust attorney who happens to be a demon, not a demon who happens to do paperwork.

## 2. History

### 2.1 The Ne-Yon casino contract
Per `apps/shared/hierarchyCanon.ts:256-261`: "the Degen is canonically the trustee of Mol'Vereth's principal under a contract authored at the Ne-Yon casino." The contract is the load-bearing canonical instrument binding Mol'Vereth to the Degen across centuries. Authoring CANNOT specify the contract's details — only that it exists, that it binds, and that its cadence is the annual audit.

### 2.2 The annual audit
"Annual audit cadence: Mol'Vereth's audits of the Degen's trusteeship have been on the same date for centuries (the anniversary of the Ne-Yon contract)." (`hierarchyCanon.ts:259-261`) This is the recurring canonical event the `mystery.the_degen` arc surfaces — each year, on the same date, Mol'Vereth audits.

### 2.3 The Hierarchy's contract-enforcement seat
Mol'Vereth's place within the Hierarchy: contract-law specialist, the demon they send when an instrument needs enforcing. Senior to floor-level enforcers (Xeth'Raal handles ledgers; Mol'Vereth handles instruments). Canonically distinct from Mol'Garath despite the surname similarity — the "Mol" prefix is a Hierarchy contract-house family marker.

## 3. Background

### 3.1 The Ne-Yon casino
The casino where the contract was authored. Canonically a contract-law venue as well as a gambling venue — agreements made at Ne-Yon tables are canonically binding under Hierarchy law in a way agreements made elsewhere are not. Mol'Vereth was at the Ne-Yon table when the instrument was signed.

### 3.2 The trusteeship structure
Mol'Vereth holds the principal; the Degen holds the trusteeship; both report to the Hierarchy's senior partners during audit cycles. The structure is canonically ASYMMETRIC — Mol'Vereth bears no risk; the Degen bears all of it. Authoring should preserve this — Mol'Vereth's amusement at the Degen's predicament is a load-bearing canonical attitude.

### 3.3 Ozhul'Vana adjacency
Cross-binds with Ozhul'Vana (senior partner, monetization-minded; appears in `mystery.the_degen` E4 threatening Coda funding). When senior-partner audits intersect with Mol'Vereth's trustee-audits, the two demons present canonically aligned — different mandates, same lien.

## 4. Cross-references

### 4.1 The Degen
The trustee. Canonical adversary-by-instrument; they are bound together at the contract level, and Mol'Vereth has no reason to wish the Degen harm beyond what the instrument prescribes. Authoring should preserve the contractual-courtesy texture — Mol'Vereth and the Degen address each other formally, like opposing counsel who have lunched together for centuries.

### 4.2 Xeth'Raal
Hierarchy CFO. Canonically Mol'Vereth's peer — Xeth'Raal handles ledgers, Mol'Vereth handles instruments. The two coordinate on multi-clause cases (`mystery.the_degen` E3 audit-into-Coda-books and `mystery.game_master` protection-contract). Functional respect, no rivalry.

### 4.3 The Coda
The Degen's professional companion / mentee. Canonically NOT a party to Mol'Vereth's instrument — the Coda is canonically uncovered, and that fact is load-bearing for `mystery.the_degen` E4 (Ozhul'Vana threatens Coda funding outside the trustee instrument's scope).

### 4.4 Mol'Garath
Surname-cousin. Canonically distinct character — Mol'Garath is the Hierarchy COO-or-equivalent; Mol'Vereth is contract-law specialist. The "Mol" prefix is a contract-house family marker, not a sibling indicator.

### 5.2 TCG presence

### 5.3 The instrument
Any document, ledger, or contract artifact appearing in `mystery.the_degen` references the Ne-Yon instrument by name. The instrument itself is canonically not depicted in full — only cited in fragments.

## 6. Canon issues

### 6.1 The instrument's full text
Canon-pending and load-bearing. The full text of the Ne-Yon contract is canonically opaque — the Degen has forgotten the page; Mol'Vereth canonically remembers but does not recite outside audit. Authoring CANNOT reveal the contract's full text.

### 6.2 The Mol'Vereth / Mol'Garath confusion
Authoring trap. They are canonically separate characters with separate functions. Cross-arc work that conflates them has violated the contract-house family-marker canon.

### 6.3 The Degen-amusement balance
Mol'Vereth's amusement at the Degen's predicament is canonical (the Degen IS the trustee; the Degen IS still paying; this is funny if you have been doing this for centuries). But the amusement is dry, never cruel. Authoring that tips into cruelty has violated the instrument-courtesy canon.

## 7. Voice samples

> "Your books are remarkable. Not in their accuracy. In their creativity. I have rarely seen a trustee improvise this many subsidiary ledgers without amending the instrument to permit them. Adjust."

> "I am not here to take what you have. I am here to confirm that what you have is, on the date of this instrument, still what the instrument says it is. Recorded."

> "The Coda is not a party. The Coda is a beneficiary. The distinction is in Section Nine. You signed Section Nine. I was there."
