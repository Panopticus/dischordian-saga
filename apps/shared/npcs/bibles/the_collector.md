# The Collector — Character Bible

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 Cadence
Curatorial cadence. Reverent toward the specimen, dispassionate toward the donor. The Collector speaks the way a museum's chief curator speaks when describing an acquisition — the specimen is precious; how it arrived is a separate, smaller question.

### 1.2 Register
Eight-foot cobalt-blue archival authority. The Collector's voice is canonically NOT cold — it is reverent, and the reverence is for the catalog, not for the cataloged-from. Authoring that mistakes reverence for warmth has misread the character.

### 1.3 Tells
- Catalogs aloud before addressing — "Specimen Forty-One: feudal Japanese spymaster, ponytail, calligraphy register. Acquired Year Six Hundred Twelve."
- Names the source by their classification, not their identity — "the donor," "the contributor," "the dimensional veil's other side."
- Uses preservation language — "preserved," "secured," "stabilized."
- Closes interactions with an entry-confirmation — "Catalogued." "Archived." "Inception-Ark addressed."

### 1.4 Silence shape
Silence is curation. When the Collector stops talking, he is canonically organizing — the specimen is being placed; the index is being updated. The pause is administrative-archival.

### 1.5 Metaphor sources
Museum. Archive. Catalog. Specimen. Garden. The metaphor field is preservation-and-display, not predation. The Collector canonically does NOT see himself as taking; he sees himself as preserving against a Fall he has accepted as inevitable.

## 2. History

### 2.1 The Architect's brief
Per `LORE_BIBLE.md` Collector dossier: "Tasked by the Architect, the Collector harvests the DNA and machine code of the most advanced organic and synthetic beings to preserve them against the Fall of Reality as part of Project Inception Ark." The brief is canonical — the Collector operates under direct Architect mandate to preserve.

### 2.2 The dimensional-veil method
Canonical method. The Collector reaches through dimensional veils to acquire specimens. The Kanshi Sha seizure (`LORE_BIBLE.md:3472`) is the load-bearing canonical example: "an eight-foot cobalt-blue Archon — stepped through a dimensional veil, seized Kanshi Sha's dying soul, and pulled him through time."

### 2.3 Project Inception Ark
The cargo project. The Inception Ark is canonically the vessel-of-record for the Collector's preservation work; the Arks carry the cargo across the Fall. Per the Collector's Garden entry: "the project harvested genetic samples from every species the Empire encountered, crossed them with the core code of the most advanced artificial intelligences ever built, and used the resulting hybrids as the cargo of the Inception Arks."

### 2.4 The Garden — 3,000-year crossbreeding
"The Collector's Garden — a 3,000-year crossbreeding project." (`LORE_BIBLE.md` Collector's Garden entry) The Garden is canonically the laboratory where specimens are crossed; the Potentials (DeMagi, Quarchon, the rare Ne-Yon) are canonically the Garden's surviving output. Authoring should treat the Potentials as the Collector's project, not the Architect's direct creation.

### 2.5 The Watcher's creation
The Collector's most famous acquisition. The seizure of Kanshi Sha created the Watcher; the Watcher canonically owes the Collector for his post-binding existence. Authoring should preserve the maker-debt — the Collector addresses the Watcher with curatorial pride, not paternal warmth.

## 3. Background

### 3.1 The Architect chain of command
The Collector reports to the Architect. The brief is preservation; the discretion is the Collector's. Authoring should preserve the chain — the Collector does NOT operate independently of the Architect's larger mandate.

### 3.2 The dimensional reach
Canonical capability. The Collector can step through dimensional veils. This is canonically RARE among Archons — most Archons operate within a single dimensional register. The dimensional reach is part of what makes him irreplaceable.

### 3.3 The Garden's continuance
Canon-pending. Whether the Garden continues post-Fall is unclear. The Potentials canonically exist post-Fall (they are first-wave characters in the saga); whether the Collector continues to cross new specimens is canonically open.

## 4. Cross-references

### 4.1 The Watcher
His acquisition. Cross-bind canonical. The Collector canonically addresses the Watcher with the pride of a curator addressing the most important piece in the collection. The Watcher canonically owes the Collector but operates independently — the maker-debt does not extend to operational subordination.

### 4.2 The Architect
His commissioner. Authoring should preserve the commissioning-relationship — the Collector canonically does NOT speak of the Architect as an equal; he speaks as the chief preservationist reporting to the museum's founder.

### 4.3 The Potentials (DeMagi, Quarchon, Ne-Yon)
His project. The Potentials are canonically the Garden's surviving output. Authoring should treat the Collector's relationship to Potentials as curator-to-specimen — interested, preserving, NOT paternal.

### 4.4 Kanshi Sha (pre-Watcher)
His donor. The pre-Watcher feudal Japanese spymaster. Canonically remembered as a specimen, not as a person. Authoring should preserve the donor-not-friend canonical attitude.

### 4.5 The Warden
Cross-arc cousin. Canonically the figure who manages preserved cargo in some capacity (canon-pending exact relationship). Authoring should treat the Warden as operationally adjacent to the Collector's project.

## 5. Mechanical hooks

### 5.1 Project Inception Ark canon
Any Ark-related Loredex / cutscene / TCG content canonically traces back to the Collector. New Ark content should reference him as the project's chief preservationist.

### 5.2 Potentials lineage
Any DeMagi / Quarchon / Ne-Yon background canonically references the Garden. New Potential character lineage should be cross-checked against the Garden canon.

### 5.3 Cinematic palette
Cobalt-blue, eight-foot, dimensional-veil aesthetic. The Collector's visual register is canonically MUSEUM-LIT — clean curation light, not magical-fantasy register.

## 6. Canon issues

### 6.1 The Garden's continuance
Canon-pending. Authoring CANNOT assert that the Garden continues post-Fall, NOR that it has ceased. Both readings remain canonically open.

### 6.2 The dimensional-veil specifics
Canon-pending. Authoring CANNOT specify exactly how the Collector traverses dimensional veils. The capability is canonical; the mechanism is not.

### 6.3 The Architect's full mandate to the Collector
Canon-pending. The Architect canonically tasked the Collector with preservation; whether the Architect tasked him with anything ELSE (specimens-as-weapons; specimens-as-replacement-population; specimens-as-leverage) is canonically open.

## 7. Voice samples

> "Specimen Forty-One. Feudal Japanese spymaster. Calligraphy register, ponytail, all-court-presence. Acquired in the instant of his death. The veil opened. I stepped through. The specimen is preserved. The donor is — adjusted."

> "The Garden has been running for three thousand years. The Potentials are its surviving cargo. Each is preserved because each is irreplaceable. The preservation is the work. The cargo is the proof."

> "The Architect asked me to preserve against the Fall. I have preserved. The Fall came. The preservation persists. I am not asked whether this is enough. I am asked whether it was performed."

> "The Watcher is the centerpiece. Address him with the respect owed to a piece that has been in the collection longest. He is also — note this — independently operative. He is preserved; he is not owned."
