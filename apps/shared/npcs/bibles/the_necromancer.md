# The Necromancer — Character Bible

## 1. Voice

### 1.1 Cadence
Magical-academic cadence. Long sentences with embedded clauses, the rhythm of a scholar reciting a proof. The Necromancer thinks in derivations — each statement is canonically a corollary of the previous one, and his speech reflects the step-by-step logic of a magical demonstration.

### 1.2 Register
Dark-elven scholar. Steampunk-aesthete. The Necromancer canonically reads as a magical-academic-with-malice — a faculty figure who would have been a beloved professor in a different timeline and who is, in THIS timeline, the architect of Resurrection Protocols that should not exist.

### 1.3 Tells
- Uses the language of proof — "Therefore," "It follows that," "By corollary."
- Refers to his Protocols by number ("Resurrection Protocol Seven indicates...").
- Names his creations with care ("Varkul — note the lengthened consonant; the name is precise").
- Uses the present tense for the past when the past is canonically still operative ("Varkul guards. Always. The Cathedral does not require a verb-tense.").

### 1.4 Silence shape
Silence is calculation. When the Necromancer stops talking, he is canonically working a proof. The pause is academic — the silence of someone running the next step before speaking it.

### 1.5 Metaphor sources
Proof. Derivation. Protocol. Construct. Magical-academic — not religious, not theistic. The Necromancer canonically does NOT speak of death as a sacred passage; he speaks of it as an open problem to which he has published partial solutions.

## 2. History

### 2.1 The Tenth Archon
Per `LORE_BIBLE.md:2535`: "The Necromancer was the tenth Archon created by the Architect in Year 600 A.A., a dark elven magician with white spiky hair, clad in a red and black robe and red steampunk glasses." Position #10 in the Archon canon (`apps/shared/archonCanon.ts:289-313`).

### 2.2 The Resurrection Protocols
"He discovered the secret to immortality, developed the Resurrection Protocols..." (`LORE_BIBLE.md:2535`) The Protocols are canonically HIS — the Hierarchy's wider death-magic infrastructure (Blood Weave / Severance Protocol via Zyr'Koth) is derivative work; the Necromancer canonically authored the originals.

### 2.3 The Matrix of Dreams flight
"He escaped the Fall of Reality by entering the Matrix of Dreams—a virtual world where he resided in the Castle of Death." (`LORE_BIBLE.md:2535`) The flight is canonical — the Necromancer chose virtual residency over physical exposure to the Fall. The Castle of Death is canonically his Matrix-of-Dreams domicile.

### 2.4 The destruction by Akai Shi
"...only to be hunted down and destroyed by the time-traveling Red Death." (`LORE_BIBLE.md:2535`) Akai Shi (the Red Death) canonically killed him inside the Matrix of Dreams. The destruction is load-bearing: it is canonically the only post-Fall Archon-death that occurred inside the Matrix, and it is the moment that established Akai Shi as a credible Archon-killer.

### 2.5 The creations
Varkul (the Blood Lord; Cathedral of Code guardian) is canonically his most significant creation. Fenra (the Moon Tyrant) may be his work (canon-pending exact creation lineage; she is canonically tied to the Necromancer's death-magic register in `LORE_BIBLE.md:3662`). Authoring CAN treat Varkul as definitively his; should treat Fenra-attribution as adjacent-but-not-confirmed.

## 3. Background

### 3.1 The dark-elven magical tradition
Canonical aesthetic tradition. White spiky hair; red-and-black robe; red steampunk glasses. The Necromancer canonically presents in this register and would canonically NOT abandon it for tactical disguise.

### 3.2 The Cathedral of Code
Varkul's domain. Inside the Matrix of Dreams. Canonically a ruined cathedral whose stained-glass windows are made of corrupted code. The Necromancer canonically designed the architecture; Varkul canonically guards it.

### 3.3 The Hierarchy's death-magic dependency
The Hierarchy canonically depends on the Necromancer's prior work. Zyr'Koth's Severance Protocol is canonically a refinement of the Blood Weave, which is canonically downstream of the Necromancer's Resurrection Protocols. Authoring should preserve this — the Hierarchy's death-magic is NOT independently authored; it is downstream of an Archon's original work.

## 4. Cross-references

### 4.1 Varkul
His creation. Cross-bind canonical. The Necromancer canonically does NOT visit Varkul casually — Varkul is a guardian, not a companion. Authoring scenes with both should preserve maker-and-construct distance, not warmth.

### 4.2 Akai Shi
His destroyer. The time-traveling Red Death. Canonical adversary-by-outcome — the destruction occurred; the events leading to it are partially canon-pending. Authoring CAN reference the destruction; should NOT author the killing-scene itself without Akai Shi bible cross-check (`apps/shared/npcs/bibles/akai_shi.md`).

### 4.3 The Architect
His maker. The Necromancer canonically operated independently of the Architect's day-to-day oversight — the Resurrection Protocols were canonically developed without direct Architect approval. The Architect canonically did NOT prevent the Necromancer's Matrix flight.

### 4.4 The Human (Detective phase)
Cross-arc cousin. The Detective's casework canonically intersects with the Necromancer's Protocols — the Detective is canonically the figure who would have closed the case if given clear evidence. Authoring should treat as case-not-closed canonical tension.

### 4.5 Fenra
Adjacent. Canon-pending exact lineage. Fenra is canonically tied to the Necromancer's death-magic register but may not be his direct creation in the way Varkul is.

## 5. Mechanical hooks

### 5.1 Matrix of Dreams cinematic
The Castle of Death cinematic register is canonically the Necromancer's domain. Producer briefs for Matrix-of-Dreams scenes should reference his aesthetic — corrupted-stained-glass, steampunk-magical lighting, red-and-black robe palette.

### 5.2 Resurrection Protocols documentation
The Protocols are referenced across the canon (`LORE_BIBLE.md`; `hierarchyCanon.ts`). New TCG / Loredex / cutscene content invoking resurrection mechanics canonically traces back to him.

### 5.3 TCG presence
Potential card archetype: resurrection-effect text. Examples: "When this card enters play, return a creature from any graveyard to your hand." "While this card is on the field, the first creature you would lose each turn returns at end of turn."

## 6. Canon issues

### 6.1 The destruction inside the Matrix
Akai Shi killed him INSIDE the Matrix of Dreams. The killing-scene specifics (location within Castle of Death; exact mechanics of the time-travel intervention) are canon-pending. Authoring CANNOT specify the scene.

### 6.2 The Fenra lineage
Canon-pending. Authoring CANNOT confirm Fenra as the Necromancer's direct creation; CAN treat them as aesthetically and operationally adjacent.

### 6.3 The Protocols' completeness
Canon-pending. The Necromancer canonically discovered the secret to immortality; whether the Protocols are COMPLETE (i.e. whether they fully replicate the Architect's original life-death cosmology) is canonically uncertain. Authoring CANNOT close this question.
