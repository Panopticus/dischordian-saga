## `room-memorial-chamber`

Hyper-realistic cinematic still, 16:9, 4K. Interior of the Memorial Chamber aboard Ark 1047 — a long solemn room approximately eleven meters deep and seven meters wide with a ceiling of four meters, more ceremonial than practical, designed as a space for remembrance rather than habitation. **The left wall and right wall** are lined with **paired memorial display bays** — six bays per side, twelve total, each bay built into the wall as a vertical recess framed in dark brass and obsidian composite, approximately 1.8 meters tall and 0.8 meters wide. Within each recess, **a holographic remembrance panel** is active: blue-white spectral portraits of fallen Eidolons rendered as soft volumetric projections hovering a few centimeters in front of matte-black backing plates. The portraits should feel ghostlike and reverent rather than technological spectacle — each one slightly different in posture and silhouette, all still, all silent. **At the base of every memorial bay**, a narrow shelf holds **a small bouquet of bioluminescent memorial flowers** — pale stems with white-blue glowing petals, some fresher, some partially wilted, the only organic softness in the room. **Dead center of the chamber** runs a narrow processional aisle of dark composite flooring, scuffed lightly by centuries of ceremonial passage but kept cleaner than the ship's utilitarian rooms. At the far end of the aisle, **a single central cenotaph pedestal** rises waist-high — dark stone-black composite with brass edge inlay, empty on its top surface except for a shallow circular recess holding a slow blue-white memorial flame made of controlled holographic light, not fire. The ceiling is otherwise dark, but **a soft diffuse blue-white spectral wash** descends from hidden recessed emitters above each memorial bay, filling the chamber with calm funerary light. No overhead practical lights are visible. The room's illumination comes from the twelve remembrance panels, the flowers' bioluminescent glow, and the cenotaph's cold memorial flame. Gentle volumetric haze in the air catches the light as thin sacred atmosphere rather than industrial fog. Materials are dark brass, obsidian composite, matte black memorial stone, and faintly reflective floor surfaces. The chamber is **the most somber and beautiful room on the Ark** — grief made architectural, respectful and held together with ritual discipline. Anamorphic lens flare on the cenotaph's blue-white flame and the brightest memorial projection. Fine film grain. Deep void-black base with blue-white spectral illumination. **No rendered text. No living people. No active holographic guides.** Cinematic 4K composition, symmetrical three-quarter wide shot from the entrance threshold at standing eye level, framing the central aisle, the paired memorial bays on both sides, and the cenotaph centered at the far end. The mood: **a room where the dead are still being remembered with such care that the room itself has become a form of prayer.**

## `room-observation-deck`

Hyper-realistic cinematic still, 16:9, 4K. Interior of the Observation Deck aboard a deep-space vessel — a circular contemplative chamber approximately fourteen meters in diameter with a domed transparent canopy rising six meters overhead. The deck is built as a quiet architectural overlook into deep space, with **the entire upper half of the room formed by a segmented transparent dome** of reinforced crystal-composite framed in thin dark brass ribs that arc upward in a radial pattern. Beyond the dome, **a vast nebular panorama** fills the sky: swirling purple gas, bruised indigo shadow bands, distant gold-lit cloud edges, and far star clusters suspended in layered depth. The cosmic view must feel immense and beautiful first, unsettling second. **Around the perimeter of the room**, a low curved observation railing of black composite and brass runs nearly the full circumference. Built into the inner perimeter are **five sparse contemplation seats** — low, elegant, widely spaced loungers upholstered in dark charcoal fabric, all facing outward toward the dome rather than toward one another. **Near the front-left quadrant of frame**, positioned beside the railing, stands **a single brass telescope on a tripod**, polished in places by long use, aimed upward through the dome toward the densest part of the nebula. Its silhouette should read clearly as the room's human-scale focal object. **On the railing beside the telescope**, a single small alien succulent sits in a shallow dark ceramic vessel — thick waxy leaves, slightly translucent, each tip glowing with a faint bioluminescent cyan-green edge. It is the only living green thing on the ship and should feel compositionally precious. The floor is dark reflective composite, almost black, with enough polish to mirror the nebular colors and dome ribs in broken reflections. At the very edges of the dome, **faint violet vortex distortions** shimmer subtly against the starfield — not a portal, not an explosion, just a quiet visual wrongness at the margins of wonder. No direct overhead fixtures are visible. Illumination comes from the nebula itself pouring purple-gold light through the dome, faint cool edge lights hidden in the dome frame, and thin reflected glow across the floor. Very light volumetric atmosphere makes the stellar light feel dimensional. Materials are glass, dark brass, black composite, and quiet luxury rather than industrial fatigue. Anamorphic lens flare on the brightest gold edge of the nebula and a subtle secondary flare on the telescope barrel. Fine film grain. Deep space black base with purple, indigo, and gold cosmological light. **No rendered text. No people. No holograms.** Cinematic 4K composition, wide three-quarter shot from the entrance side of the chamber at standing eye level, framing the telescope in the left foreground, the curved seating around the perimeter, the reflective floor in the lower half, and the dome's cosmic vista dominating the upper half. The mood: **the most peaceful room on the ship, built for wonder, now carrying the first faint sign that wonder is looking back.**

## `room-purification-chamber`

Hyper-realistic cinematic still, 16:9, 4K. Interior of the Purification Chamber within the Medical Bay complex — a small luminous sanctum approximately five meters square with a ceiling of three meters, nested behind clinical bulkheads but designed as a ritual-medical space where healing and reverence overlap. **Dead center of the room** stands **a single white crystal pedestal** rising from the floor on a circular base of pale composite and brushed brass, the pedestal approximately 1.2 meters tall, faceted and translucent, internally lit with a warm golden-green radiance. Above and around the pedestal, **the Dreamer's shield frequency** is visible as **a semi-transparent harmonic dome** — a soft hemispheric field of golden-green light suspended in the air, beginning thirty centimeters above the floor and curving overhead to just below the ceiling. Within the dome, delicate wavefront patterns, concentric ripples, and faint lattice harmonics shimmer in the air as if sound has become visible. The effect should feel mathematically clean, sacred, and medically purposeful rather than mystical chaos. **The floor** is sterile pale composite with a circular inlay directly beneath the pedestal — a thin brass ring and four subtle radial marks aligned to the room's cardinal axes. **The back wall** holds three recessed alcoves containing sealed medical-spiritual instruments: shallow trays, folded white filtration cloths, and small dark brass diagnostic housings, all arranged with ceremonial precision. **The side walls** are smooth white composite with faint gold-green edge seams and two narrow vertical light recesses per wall, their glow softened to keep the chamber calm rather than bright. No clutter. No dirt. No visible damage. The room should read as the cleanest, most intentionally maintained space in the surrounding medical deck. Illumination comes from the crystal pedestal, the golden-green harmonic dome, and the soft wall recess lights; the entire chamber is wrapped in diffuse sacred warmth with pale green undertones. Very faint atmospheric particulate makes the harmonic wave patterns visible without making the room feel foggy. Materials are white crystal, pale ceramic composite, brushed brass, sterile glass, and soft medical light. Anamorphic lens flare on the crystal pedestal core and a softer bloom along the upper arc of the harmonic dome. Fine film grain. Palette anchored in white, pale gold, and green-gold frequency light against restrained shadow. **No rendered text. No people. No holograms beyond the shield-frequency dome itself.** Cinematic 4K composition, symmetrical three-quarter wide shot from the doorway at standing eye level, framing the pedestal dead center, the harmonic dome filling most of the room volume, and the recessed wall alcoves as calm secondary detail. The mood: **a room where medicine has been refined into ritual, and ritual has been disciplined until it can hold back darkness.**

## `room-summoning-chamber`

Hyper-realistic cinematic still, 16:9, 4K. Interior of the Summoning Chamber inside the Castle of Death — a ceremonial ritual space approximately ten meters across with a ceiling rising into shadow at nearly five meters, where necromantic grandeur and industrial Hierarchy design have fused into one deliberate architecture. **The floor** is dominated by **an elaborate ritual circle** carved directly into dark stone and blackened composite, roughly six meters in diameter, its geometry composed of concentric rings, radial spokes, angular sigils, and embedded corporate-Hierarchy motifs worked seamlessly into the occult patterning. Every carved channel in the circle glows with **foxfire green #00e676**, the light strongest at the center and dimmer at the outer geometry, as if power is being drawn inward. **At the exact center of the circle** lies a low summoning dais of black stone and brass inlay, empty for the still image, with faint green vapor curling across its surface. **Around the perimeter of the chamber**, twelve heavy brass candlestands stand at uneven ritual intervals, each holding a green-flame candle whose fire is narrow, vertical, and unnaturally still. **The walls** are a hybrid of dark bone architecture and red industrial steampunk construction: rib-like ossified buttresses rising between panels of rust-red metal, with brass pipes, valve wheels, and pressure conduits threading between them. The bone forms should feel structural, not decorative — as though the chamber was grown and engineered at the same time. **On the far wall**, a tall recessed niche holds a black altar-console with brass controls and a small array of dim red and green status lamps, suggesting that the ritual can be monitored, measured, and corporately sanctioned. The floor outside the circle is worn stone dusted with ash and faint pale residue from prior ceremonies. The air is heavy with thin green smoke and low red atmospheric warmth, but the green runes remain the dominant light source. No natural light enters. Illumination comes from the foxfire runes, the green-flame candles, a few dim red industrial indicators, and soft reflected brass highlights along the pipes and fittings. Anamorphic lens flare on the brightest rune intersections and subtle bloom on the green candle flames. Fine film grain. Palette anchored in black stone, bone ivory, rust-red metal, brass, foxfire green, and restrained infernal crimson. **No rendered text. No visible people. No summoned entity.** Cinematic 4K composition, high three-quarter wide shot from just inside the chamber entrance at standing eye level, framing the full ritual circle in the lower middle, the altar-console on the far wall, and enough of the ribbed bone-and-pipe architecture to sell the chamber as both occult and institutional. The mood: **a room where death has been formalized, audited, and made beautiful enough that terror begins to resemble devotion.**

## `room-trophy-room`

Hyper-realistic cinematic still, 16:9, 4K. Interior of the Trophy Room — a prestigious exhibition hall approximately fifteen meters deep and nine meters wide with a ceiling of four and a half meters, designed with the ceremonial gravity of a private museum and the ego of a victory shrine. **A central procession aisle** runs from the entrance toward the far wall across a polished dark stone floor with subtle brass inlay lines guiding the eye inward. **On both sides of the aisle**, a disciplined sequence of illuminated display plinths and glass vitrines forms two parallel rows: six pedestal stations per side, twelve primary showcase positions total. Each pedestal is black composite with brass trim and a dedicated top spotlight, while each vitrine is clear armored glass with faint edge illumination. The trophies themselves should vary in silhouette and material — polished cups, ceremonial helms, broken enemy standards, engraved medallions, lacquered relic boxes, crystal awards, and abstract victory sculptures — but all arranged with curatorial exactness. Nothing should feel cluttered; each object is given space to imply status. **The side walls** hold **holographic achievement plaques** mounted in evenly spaced vertical intervals — translucent cyan-white panels hovering a few centimeters off matte-black wall panels, each plaque carrying the visual structure of commendation text, seals, and insignia without rendering any legible words. Their glow should feel expensive, archival, and institutional. **The far wall** is the visual anchor: a raised semicircular dais reached by two shallow steps, supporting **the room's dominant master trophy** on a waist-high central pedestal under the brightest warm-gold spotlight in the chamber. The master trophy should be large, sculptural, and unmistakably important — a hybrid of metallic laurels, dark crystal, and brass filigree, with enough complexity to read as the culmination of a career or dynasty. Ceiling-mounted spotlights cast controlled cones of gold light onto the pedestals and vitrines, while the holographic plaques add cool cyan edge contrast around the room. Reflections should play across the polished floor and glass cases in restrained museum fashion. The room is immaculate, almost unnervingly so — no dust, no damage, no casual disorder, only curated triumph. Anamorphic lens flare on the far master trophy spotlight and a softer flare on the brightest side-wall holographic plaque. Fine film grain. Palette anchored in black stone, dark composite, brass, warm gold spotlights, and cool cyan holographic accents. **No rendered text. No people. No holograms except the static plaque displays.** Cinematic 4K composition, centered three-quarter wide shot from the entrance at standing eye level, framing the full procession aisle, the twin rows of pedestal displays, the holographic plaques along both walls, and the master trophy commanding the far end of the hall. The mood: **a room built to preserve achievement so perfectly that admiration and intimidation become the same feeling.**
