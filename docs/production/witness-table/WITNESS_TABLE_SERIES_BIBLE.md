# The Witness Table — Dischordia Lore Podcast Series Bible

Oct 29, 2026 · @Panopticus

## The show

**The Witness Table** is a two-host lore podcast in which two fans break down the Dischordian Saga, era by era, and track the real-world rise of AI power alongside it. It runs as a video podcast on @thedischordia, 25–35 minutes per episode, in the long-form narrated lore format that Warhammer 40K, Dark Souls and Destiny audiences binge.

**Why the name.** In *No Matter Where You Go*, the resistance forms around a table with a spare chair: there is always room for one more. The hosts sit at their own table with an empty third chair, which stands for the viewer. Two people testifying across a long stretch of time also quietly echoes the Two Witnesses. The hosts notice this in Episode 1 and refuse to take it seriously, which is the joke.

**The fiction layer.** The hosts are fans who found the saga on TikTok, lurked in the Discord, then started playing and writing CoNexus games. Since then, small things have been going wrong. Each season escalates the question "are we inside a CoNexus story?" without ever fully answering it.

**The real-world layer.** Each episode includes **Signal Check**, a segment pairing the episode's lore theme with real, sourced AI and policy news: surveillance, data brokers, deepfakes, compute and energy, AI in government and elections.

### Rules for the real-world segment

1. **Receipts, not rumors.** Every real-world claim is named with its outlet and date on screen, and the links go in the description. A claim with a single source is called a single-source claim.
2. **Lore and receipts stay labelled.** The hosts never say a fictional event is real. They say "in the saga" and "in our world" out loud and keep the two separate.
3. **Systems, not scapegoats.** The subject is how power concentrates: corporations, governments, platforms, incentives. No theories that blame an ethnic, religious or national group, no "secret cabal" framing, no health misinformation.
4. **Even-handed on politics.** Criticize policies and documented actions on every side with the same standard of evidence. Report what officials did and said; don't tell viewers how to vote.
5. **Conspiracy means documented misconduct.** Lean on things that were proven: court records, regulator orders, investigative reporting, leaked documents later confirmed. Speculation is labelled as speculation.
6. **Refresh before recording.** News items in a script are placeholders as of the writing date. Re-verify them or swap in that week's headlines before the session.
7. **Real people in the saga's videos.** When a video depicts a real person, give the facts about them from sources, quote only what they actually said, and say on air that the depiction is artistic (and AI-generated, if it is). Fan comments are anonymized or used with permission.

### The dynamic

- **Evidence versus instinct.** Ko asks "what's the source?" and Jun asks "what if?" The best moments are when his instinct turns out to have a source and she has to admit it.
- **Shippable, not dating.** They are best friends with very good timing. They finish each other's references, not each other's sentences. Neither one acknowledges the comment section asking whether they're dating; Jun once read a fan edit of them on air and Ko left the room. Small tells for shippers: she always has his favorite snack; he keeps her pen when she loses it. Never confirm, never deny.
- **Running bits.** Jun calls her "sunbae"; she calls him "Trainee." The Empty Chair, where Jun sets a third mug "just in case." Ko's red-string board with a citation on every string. Jun rating each lore character's idol potential out of ten.
- **What they protect.** Both treat the real-world segment seriously. When Signal Check starts, the jokes drop for a minute.
