## Findings

**1. The Archives Data-Banks Hotspot Is a Mise-en-Abyme — Elara Watching the Editor Watch Her**
- **Severity:** P0 (load-bearing to the entire conspiracy theme)
- **Where:** `apps/shared/roomMysteries/archives.ts:60-100` (`data-banks` look response, tiers 0-2)
- **What's wrong:** The player's third Look on the data-banks hotspot reveals that Elara has been unable to perceive a permission tier she created herself, and has been **actively editing her own unreadability out of her own awareness.** But the structure here is not just horror — it's a WINK. The game shows Elara discovering she's been unaware of her own blindness, which is *exactly* how the player discovers they haven't been reading what's in front of them. The "fourteen thousand edits" clue (order:2) mentions the Editor wrote "a small novel" on top of her records **using her prose rhythm and turns of phrase.** This is player-facing framing: *you* are reading that same overwritten ledger and you don't know it yet.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** This is a Kojima move. The room doesn't just convey lore; it **confesses the medium.** archives.ts:101-131 (the `"novel-overwrite"` tier) then explicitly tells the player: "The book is, by length and consistency, finished. We are reading the published edition. We can probably find the manuscript." The unfinished sentence structure — *can probably* — is leaving a door open that the codebase does not close.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Add a hidden-discovery hook in `getRoomMysteryModule()` (`apps/shared/roomMysteries/index.ts:105-107`) that tags the `data-banks` response with a `discoveryGate: "player_can_perceive_this"` flag; (2) Create a new room mystery module `apps/shared/roomMysteries/manuscriptVault.ts` that surfaces only after the player has logged both `clue-archives-novel-overwrite` *and* a specific other clue from bridge/cryo/med-bay that the Editor would have had access to; (3) Extend the `CIVILOPEDIA_INDEX` in `apps/shared/civilopedia.ts` to include a `manuscript_fragment` category entry pointing to the vault location — let wanons manually connect the dots on the conspiracy board before the game does.

**2. Hard-Coded Riddle Answers Hide in Plain Type Variance**
- **Severity:** P1 (intentional, but exploitable by speedrunners)
- **Where:** `apps/client/src/components/PuzzleSystem.tsx:64, :76, :104, :116` (four answer fields)
- **What's wrong:** The riddle answers are:
  - "lore" (archives puzzle, line 64)
  - "courage" (armory puzzle, line 104)
  - "the saga continues" (comms cipher, line 76)
  - "cargo hold open" (cargo cipher, line 116)

  The first three are *thematic to the Dischordian Saga's cosmology.* "Lore" is the fabric binding all stories (from the riddle's own text). "Courage" ties to the Warden's obsession with power. But "the saga continues" appears *nowhere* in the riddle text itself — it is a hidden reference to the fact that the player is playing the saga that continues. And "cargo hold open" is a reversed instruction to the player: you are opening a hold. You are the cargo. The codebase understands the riddles as **player-facing confessions,** not world-contained puzzles.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** In Tunic, the manual pages hidden in the world *are the game teaching you how to read the game.* These riddles are doing the same thing. The acceptableAnswers arrays (line 65, :105) give breathing room, but the canonical answer in the `answer` field is *which* interpretation the author chose. That choice is a message.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Add a new Clue type `"metariddle"` to `CLUE_TYPE_META` in `ClueJournal.tsx:391-397`; (2) Tag the comms-array and archives puzzles with a `meta: true` flag in `PuzzleSystem.tsx` that surfaces in the Clue Journal as a separate section ("TRANSMISSIONS ABOUT THE GAME") once all room clues are logged; (3) Create `apps/shared/riddleCommentary.ts` with per-riddle author notes on *why* each answer was chosen — make the notes discoverable only after solving the riddle, so the player cannot skip straight to the meta layer.

**3. ClueJournal Post-PR #510 Does Not Collapse Its Own Variants Into a Single Narrative**
- **Severity:** P2 (missed opportunity, not a bug)
- **Where:** `apps/client/src/components/ClueJournal.tsx:458-461` (journalVariant resolver)
- **What's wrong:** The journal surface variant (`journal_act{N}_page`) is resolved per act and rendered once at the top of the log, but it is **a flat list below it.** The player's act-specific mood tone is cosmetic window-dressing. The `ClueJournal.tsx:471-485` filteredClues logic sorts by source and order, never by narrative act or by the player's morality band. If the player collected the same three clues in Act 1 (as "balanced") and Act 5 (as "machine"), they see the same clue text twice, in the same order, with no indication that *they changed how they see that evidence.*
- **Why it matters to this persona:** Outer Wilds hides its secrets in *what the Nomai don't tell you* — the blank spaces in the logs. The journal is doing the opposite: it is telling you everything and hiding the fact that you interpret it differently at each pass. The variant system (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts:42-66`) has the infrastructure (morality, trust, act gates), but ClueJournal doesn't consume it.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Add a `variants?: MoralityTrustActVariant[]` field to the Clue interface in `apps/shared/roomMysteries/_template.ts`; (2) Modify `getAllAuthoredClues()` (`index.ts:113-135`) to also walk the VARIANT_REGISTRY and attach matching variants to each logged clue; (3) In `ClueJournal.tsx`, when expanding a clue card (line 677-681), check if the clue has variants matching the player's current (morality, trust, act) and render them as a secondary panel: "HOW YOU READ THIS THEN" vs "HOW YOU READ THIS NOW." This turns the journal into a personal mirror.

**4. LOREDEX Discovery State Leaks Into the Graph Renderer But Not Into Itself**
- **Severity:** P1 (architectural inconsistency)
- **Where:** `apps/client/src/contexts/LoredexContext.tsx:243-250` (discoverEntry); `apps/client/src/pages/LoredexGraphPage.tsx:44-66` (focusNeighbourhood); `apps/shared/loredexGraph.ts:121-200` (focusNeighbourhood pure helper)
- **What's wrong:** The `focusNeighbourhood()` function accepts a `discoveredIds` set (line 125) and uses it to fog undiscovered neighbours (line 174-183). But the LOREDEX relationship model (`apps/client/src/contexts/LoredexContext.tsx:46-53`, Relationship interface) has no discovery-gating at the relationship level — only at the entry level. This means: if the player discovers "entity_kael" (Kael the Suspect) and "entity_elara" (Elara), the game will render the edge between them *even if* the relationship "kael_shadow_tongue_collaboration" should only be visible *after* the player logs the clue `clue-archives-two-users` (line 104 in archives.ts). The graph confidently shows connections the player hasn't earned the right to see yet.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** In Tunic, the manual tells you the controls BEFORE you have the ability to execute them — and the realization that you were reading ahead the whole time is the shock. The LOREDEX is doing the opposite: it hides the node (Kael) until you discover him, but once both nodes are visible, *every edge appears instantly.* The player never gets the moment of "wait, these entities were always connected and I just didn't see it."
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Extend the Relationship interface in `LoredexContext.tsx:46-53` to include `discoveryGate?: string` (a clue id or narrative flag that must be true for the edge to render); (2) Modify `focusNeighbourhood()` in `loredexGraph.ts` to accept an additional `discoveredClues: Set<string>` parameter and filter edges that fail their gate; (3) Add a new "REDACTED CONNECTIONS" section to `LoredexGraphPage.tsx` that shows "? connections from this entity remain hidden" with a count, so the player knows they're not seeing the full graph yet.

**5. The Five LOREDEX Clusters Are Tagged But Not Cross-Indexed**
- **Severity:** P2 (infrastructure planted, game not using it)
- **Where:** `apps/shared/loredexClusters.ts:10-35` (cluster definitions); `apps/shared/loredexGraph.ts` (no cluster awareness); `apps/client/src/data/loredex-data.json` (cluster field tagged on concept entries)
- **What's wrong:** Five clusters exist (imprint_mechanics, witnessing_doctrine, audit_discipline, seer_method, lionism_ethics). The `tallyConceptClusters()` function (line 47-58) counts them. But the graph viewer (`LoredexGraphPage.tsx`) has zero cluster rendering. The nodes are colour-coded by *entry type* (character / concept / event), not by cluster. A "concept" tagged with cluster:"seer_method" renders the same colour as a "concept" tagged with cluster:"lionism_ethics." The player sees the cluster metadata exists (it's in the JSON), but the UI gives no way to *navigate* by cluster or to understand why those five groupings matter.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** The five clusters are thematic mirrors. "Seer Method" (knowledge of what will be) and "Audit Discipline" (knowledge of what was) are inverted epistemologies. "Imprint Mechanics" is the *how* of consciousness transfer. "Witnessing Doctrine" and "Lionism Ethics" are orthogonal moral systems. The game has baked the skeleton of a conspiracy board (five pillars) into the data, but hasn't surfaced it. A wanon could spend 40 hours mapping connections and never realize the five clusters are *the* map.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Create `apps/client/src/components/LoredexClusterView.tsx` that renders the 1-hop graph *grouped by cluster* instead of by type — each cluster gets a tab or toggle; (2) Add a "CLUSTER FILTER" panel to `LoredexGraphPage.tsx` that lets players toggle clusters on/off, automatically surfacing which cluster(s) a focused entity belongs to; (3) In ClueJournal, add a "THEMATIC THREADS" tab that queries the Civilopedia index by cluster and surfaces "What did you learn about Seer Method this act?" — turn discovery into reflection.

**6. The Bridge Conspiracy Board's Blank Pin + Violet Thread Is a Pedagogical Exploit Hiding in Room-Mystery Flavor Text**
- **Severity:** P0 (intentional, but the teaching mechanism isn't portable)
- **Where:** `apps/shared/roomMysteries/bridge.ts:40-132` (tactical-display hotspot, tiers 0-2, nested narration structures)
- **What's wrong:** The three-tier Look on the tactical-display builds a secret history: (T0) three threads point at a blank pin, the crew refused to name the suspect; (T1) there is a *fourth* thread, violet, connecting the pin to ELARA-SYS — the crew suspected Elara herself; (T2) the pin was never blank, it's covered in the "unreadable hue" the player first encountered in the cryo bay. By tier 2, the player has learned: (a) the shadow tongue edits reality in a specific colour, (b) Elara cannot perceive that colour, (c) **the previous crew left evidence in a form Elara cannot see and the current player cannot read without Elara's help.** This is *teaching the player the game's core mechanic* — you are a detective reading a crime scene that the victim's own memory is obscuring. The three narrations (lucid / fragmented / luminous) are per-human-state, not per-discovery-tier. A player in a different mental state reads a different version of the same clue.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** In Outer Wilds, every puzzle is also a lesson in how to interpret puzzles. The Bridge is doing exactly that. But bridge.ts is a single room module. There are 26 room modules. The narration multiplexing (lucid/fragmented/luminous) is **custom to bridge.ts's hotspot responses.** It doesn't port to other rooms without copy-paste. The game has discovered a pedagogical pattern (tier-gated discovery + multistate narration + shadow-tongue-hue-as-visual-cipher) and locked it inside a single room.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Extract the three-narration pattern into a shared RoomMysteryModule feature: `tierResponses?: { narration: { lucid, fragmented, luminous }, logsClue?, setsFlag? }[]` in `apps/shared/roomMysteries/_template.ts`; (2) Refactor `bridge.ts`'s `tiers` array into the new structure; (3) Add a "NARRATIVE STATE" panel to ClueJournal that shows the player's current lucid/fragmented/luminous score (derived from `moralityScore` or a new `mentalState` flag) and renders *all* collected clues in their current narrative voice — suddenly the entire journal rewrites itself if the player's mental state shifts; (4) Document this in `docs/design/AUTHORING_MULTISTATE_CLUES.md` so future room authors use it.

**7. Sealed Letters & Burnt Cards in Hotspot Responses Are Story Flavor, Not ARG Entry Points**
- **Severity:** P3 (low-priority signal, but the absence is the signal)
- **Where:** `apps/shared/roomMysteries/*.ts` (grep across all 26 rooms for `"burnt"` / `"sealed"` / `"letter"`); e.g., `captains-quarters.ts`, `medicalBay.ts`, `cargoHold.ts`
- **What's wrong:** Every room mystery has hotspots with richly authored narration about documents that *cannot be examined further.* A burnt card reads as "too damaged." A sealed letter reads as "your hands cannot open it." These are narrative beats, not puzzles. But they are *also* a wall — the player is told repeatedly that they cannot access information, which conditions them to assume unreachable content is **unavailable on purpose.** In *Tunic*, the mangled pages are the game teaching you that some knowledge is legible only at certain angles. Here, sealed letters are a constant reminder that **there is content you cannot reach**, which makes a player trust that if the game wanted them to find something, it would be reachable.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** This is a conspiracy theorist's *trap.* It's a confidence game. The game trains you to accept walls. Then, when the *real* hidden content (the Archives' fourteen-thousand-edit novel, the Bridge's fourth violet thread) appears, you trust it's actually there because the game has earned credibility by *not* lying about the inaccessible stuff. The sealed letters and burnt cards are the price of admission to the conspiracy. They teach you that the game respects boundaries.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Don't change them; (2) instead, add a post-game "ACCESSIBLE UNREACHABLES" registry in `apps/shared/inaccessibleContent.ts` that catalogs which sealed letters / burnt cards appear in which rooms, their positions, and a `maybeAccessibleAt?: string` field (a future act or discovery condition); (3) after the player completes the main arc, surface a "RECONSTRUCTION" tab in ClueJournal that attempts to reconstruct the content of burnt cards from contextual clues in surrounding hotspots — turn the inaccessibility into a *secondary puzzle.* This makes the wall a door that only appears when you look back.

**8. Puzzle Chains Are Gated But Not Visualized As a Dependency Graph**
- **Severity:** P2 (navigation fog)
- **Where:** `apps/client/src/components/ClueJournal.tsx:732-809` (Investigations tab, active puzzle chains); `apps/client/src/game/adventureFeatures.ts` (PUZZLE_CHAINS definition, not in audit scope but referenced at line 18)
- **What's wrong:** The Investigations tab shows active chains in linear list form (current step / total steps). But `PUZZLE_CHAINS` is a flat array. If Chain A's final step requires completing Chain B first, the player sees no visual connection between the two chains. They must infer the dependency by comparing the chains' reward conditions or by reading the step descriptions. The ClueJournal surface does not render a *graph* of which chains unlock which chains.
- **Why it matters to this persona:** Outer Wilds hides knowledge of the time loop *inside the time loop* — you cannot see the loop until you have died enough times to triangulate it. The puzzle chain system is doing the same thing: you cannot see the shape of the investigation *until you have failed enough threads* to understand their dependencies. But *Outer Wilds makes the loop visible once you understand it.* The ClueJournal never does — it remains a flat list even after you have completed every chain.
- **Recommended fix:** (1) Add a `prerequisiteChains?: string[]` field to the PuzzleChain interface; (2) Modify `getActivePuzzleChains()` to respect prerequisites (do not mark a chain as active until its prerequisites are complete); (3) Add a "INVESTIGATION DIAGRAM" view to ClueJournal that renders the chains as nodes and prerequisites as directed edges — once the player understands the shape of the investigation, let them see it drawn.

## The pinboard I want

The current Investigations tab is a **step counter.** What I want is a **conspiracy board for the player's own deductions.** Imagine: after completing any puzzle chain, the ClueJournal surfaces a "THREAD AVAILABLE" button. The player pins it to a blank conspiracy board (separate from the in-universe Ark Bridge board). As they log more clues, the system suggests connections: *"You have logged clues from three different rooms mentioning the Panopticon's founding year. Pin a thread?"* Clicking "yes" auto-draws a red string between those three clue cards. By end-game, the player's personal board is a web of their own discoveries, auto-generated from their ClueJournal.

The wiring model already exists: `ClueJournal.tsx` uses variant resolution (`moralityTrustActVariants.ts`) to surface act-specific mood text. Extend that same resolver to *clue relationships:* if the player is in the "machine" morality band, suggest connections a "humanity" player would miss. This turns discovery into a mirror of the player's choices, not a fixed checklist.

**Effort estimate:** 3-4 days (UI component for free-form pinboard + variant resolver extension + relationship suggestion engine + localStorage persistence for the player's board state). The hardest part is the suggestion engine; the infrastructure is already shipped.
